Adjunct and visiting lecturer roles in Sweden: the next step after guest lecturing

Written by Ted Scheiman, banking and fintech executive, guest lecturer. Last reviewed 17 November 2026. How we research and fact-check

You have given guest lectures in the same course for a few years. The teacher likes your sessions, the students rate them, and someone mentions that you could become "adjungerad". What does that actually mean?

In Sweden, an adjunct appointment (adjungering) is a formal, part-time university employment for someone whose main job is outside higher education. It is not an honorary title and not a guest lecture. It comes with rules on scope, duration and qualifications, and universities publish their own local rules on top of the national framework.

This guide explains the adjunct and visiting roles at Swedish universities, using rules published by Uppsala, Lund, Gothenburg, Stockholm and KTH, and a collective agreement for state employers. It is general information, not legal or HR advice. Always check the current rules at the university you are talking to.

Key takeaways

  • An adjunct role is part-time university employment for a person whose main work stays outside higher education [1] [3].
  • Adjunct professors can be employed for at most twelve years in total under the Higher Education Ordinance [1] [2].
  • Typical scope is around 20% of full time. Published local limits range up to just under 50% [2] [4] [6].
  • Visiting professors (gästprofessor) are a different category, usually for people employed at another university, for up to five years [2] [5].
  • The route in is a long relationship with a department. Guest lectures are often where that starts.

What "adjungerad" means in Swedish rules

The Swedish Higher Education Ordinance (högskoleförordningen, SFS 1993:100) regulates the main teacher categories. Uppsala University's guidelines quote its chapter 4, section 11, on adjunct professors: the total employment period may cover at most twelve years [1]. Uppsala's social sciences faculty adds that visiting professors may be employed for at most five years, under chapter 4, section 12 [2].

For adjunct senior lecturers and lecturers, the detailed terms come from a central collective agreement for state employers on fixed-term employment of adjunct teachers. The joint commentary from 2011 says the purpose is to bring in teachers whose main expertise lies outside the higher education sector. The person's main employment must be outside the sector, usually a permanent job with another employer, though self-employment can also qualify. A typical appointment is about 20% of full time, for at most two years per period, renewable while the need remains [3]. Lund University confirms that its adjunct lecturer posts are governed by this agreement [5].

Chalmers is a useful reminder that local practice varies. It lists "visiting, adjunct and affiliated positions" as one category in its academic career structure [8]. In January 2026 it announced that a Volvo Group IP strategy leader had been appointed part-time adjunct senior lecturer for 2025 to 2028, to help shape a new advanced-level course [9].

How universities apply the rules

Each university adds its own rules in an appointment regulation (anställningsordning) or guidelines. Some examples:

  • KTH: an adjunct professor works at least 20% and at most 50% of full time, for at most twelve years in total. The initiative can come from the main employer, from the person, or from the KTH research group, and the post is funded by the home organisation or other external funds [4].
  • Uppsala University: an adjunct professor's main work must be outside higher education. Scope is normally about 20%, and up to 49% in some cases. A written agreement with the main employer covers tasks, costs, ownership of results, publication and confidentiality [2]. The university's guidelines say employment is limited to three years at a time and can be renewed [1].
  • Lund University: adjunct posts are always part-time, at most 50%, and mainly focused on teaching. Research or supervision may be included [5].
  • University of Gothenburg: adjunct professors and adjunct lecturers are normally appointed at 20–40%, always below 50%, and must have their main professional activity outside higher education. Adjunct senior lecturers and lecturers must complete teacher training or development during their first two years to continue [6].
  • Stockholm University, Faculty of Science: the most common scope for an adjunct professor is 20%. The principal employer pays the salary, and the person is employed by the university at a salary of 0 SEK [7].

That last point is common in practice: the adjunct role is often funded by your employer, not by the university. Uppsala's guidelines describe a three-party agreement between the university, the main employer and the adjunct [1].

What qualifications you need

Requirements follow the matching full-time category, with some adjustments:

  • Adjunct professor: at Gothenburg, scientific and teaching skill are both required [6]. At Uppsala's social sciences faculty, the usual professor requirements apply except for teaching skill [2]. Stockholm's science faculty expects associate professor (docent) level or equivalent, plus active research and supervision experience [7].
  • Adjunct senior lecturer: at Gothenburg, teaching skill plus a doctorate, equivalent competence or other relevant skill [6].
  • Adjunct lecturer: at Gothenburg, teaching skill plus a degree at least at advanced level, or equivalent [6].

For most professionals without a doctorate, the realistic adjunct roles are therefore adjunct lecturer or adjunct senior lecturer, not adjunct professor.

How professionals get there

Adjunct posts are usually created because a department has a need. Uppsala's process starts when the head of department sends a request explaining the need, the duties and a financing plan [2]. Stockholm's science faculty also says the department initiates [7]. In other words, someone inside has to want you.

A realistic path looks like this:

  1. Teach regularly in the same department. Repeated guest lectures in one course show that you can teach and that students value it.
  2. Contribute beyond lectures. Supervising a thesis, providing a case or co-developing a course module builds a record.
  3. Talk to your employer early. Many adjunct roles depend on your employer releasing and paying for your time [1] [7].
  4. Ask the head of department or programme director whether an adjunct role is possible, and what the local rules are.

Frequently asked questions

Can I become an adjunct lecturer without a PhD?

Possibly. At the University of Gothenburg, an adjunct lecturer (adjungerad universitetsadjunkt) needs teaching skill and a degree at least at advanced level, or equivalent competence [6]. Adjunct professor roles normally require professor-level qualifications.

How much time does an adjunct role take?

Often about 20% of full time. KTH allows 20–50% for adjunct professors, and Gothenburg normally uses 20–40% [4] [6].

Who pays an adjunct?

It varies. At Stockholm University's science faculty the principal employer pays the salary [7], and KTH says posts are funded by the home organisation or other external funds [4]. Ask the department how the post would be financed.

What is the difference between an adjunct and a visiting professor?

An adjunct professor's main work is outside higher education. A visiting professor (gästprofessor) is usually employed at another university and can be appointed for at most five years [2] [5].

Is "professor of practice" a Swedish title?

The Swedish rules we reviewed do not use it. The closest Swedish category for an industry expert is usually adjunct professor (adjungerad professor) [4].
