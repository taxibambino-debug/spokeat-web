Online public speaking and presentation courses

Written by Ted Scheiman, banking and fintech executive, guest lecturer. Last reviewed 16 November 2026. How we research and fact-check

You want to train your speaking, but a weekly evening class in town does not fit your diary. Or you live far from a university city. Online courses solve the travel problem. They also raise a fair question: can you learn to speak in a room by sitting at a laptop?

This directory lists online and distance options we could verify on each provider's page, from Swedish university distance courses with credits to short private courses and speaking clubs that accept online attendance. Every listing was checked on 16 November 2026.

Most of these courses run in Swedish. Where a course is taught in English, we say so.

Key takeaways

  • Linköping University runs several rhetoric courses as distance courses at quarter pace, including one on speaking and one aimed at people who speak at work.
  • Malmö University runs a five-week distance course in online presentation skills, taught in English.
  • University courses are free for citizens of EU/EEA countries and Switzerland; fees for others are listed per course.
  • NobleProg runs presentation courses as online live training, with some dates and prices published.
  • International platforms such as Coursera, edX and FutureLearn host self-paced public speaking courses in English from universities. Some are free to take; certificates usually cost extra.
  • Toastmasters' club finder can filter for clubs that accept online attendance.

What "online" means in practice

The word covers very different formats. Check which one you are buying.

Format What it looks like Example in this directory
Asynchronous distance course Recorded lectures, deadlines, presentations submitted or given online Malmö University AK107E uses pre-recorded lectures and oral presentations with digital media [6]
Distance course over a full term Work spread across a term at low pace; check the course page for any scheduled sessions Linköping University's distance speaking course lists blended teaching time [1]
Live online short course One to three days on video with a trainer NobleProg's online live courses of 7 to 21 hours [8]
Self-paced online course (MOOC) Videos, readings and exercises on a platform, at your own pace University of Washington's Introduction to Public Speaking on Coursera [15]
Club with online attendance Regular meetings you can join remotely Toastmasters clubs flagged for online attendance [11]

A practical tip: speaking to a camera and speaking to a room are related but different skills. If your goal is to stand on a stage, pair an online course with live practice. Our guide to guest teaching online covers how to use online lectures as practice once you have the basics.

Notes on each course: - 738G33 is aimed at students and professionals, such as teachers, communicators and managers, who present, inform or speak in different settings [2]. Linköping University describes it as practical tools in rhetoric, presentation technique and communication for working life [5]. - 738G42 is the distance version of Linköping's introductory speaking course. It requires general entry requirements, and selection uses grades, the Swedish university aptitude test and credits [1]. - 738G37 requires a passed introductory rhetoric course of 7.5 credits, or Swedish language text analysis of 7.5 credits or equivalent [3]. - 738G30 is a writing course. We include it because persuasive writing and speaking share the same rhetorical basics, and Linköping groups it with its rhetoric courses [4] [5]. - AK107E is designed for university students who want to master online academic presentations. Activities include literature studies, pre-recorded lectures, supervised exercises and oral presentations with digital media [6]. The syllabus lists English as the language of instruction [7].

International online platforms (self-paced)

These courses come from universities abroad and are taught in English. You study at your own pace, with no live audience. Use them for structure and theory, and add live practice elsewhere.

How to choose an online course

Ask four questions before you sign up:

  1. Will I speak live to other people? A course where you only upload recordings trains a different skill from a course with live audiences.
  2. Who gives feedback, and how often? Small groups allow more individual feedback.
  3. Does the timing fit? Quarter pace over a full term suits steady practice. A short intensive course suits a deadline, such as a talk next month.
  4. Do I need credits? If you want credits on your transcript, choose a university course and watch the application deadlines.

For a fuller comparison of courses, clubs and coaches, read how to choose a public speaking course.

Frequently asked questions

Can you learn public speaking online?

You can learn structure, argument and delivery online, and practise on camera with feedback. A camera and a live room are different settings, though. If your goal is live talks, pair the online course with live practice, such as a club or a short talk at work.

Is there a free online rhetoric course in Sweden?

Swedish university distance courses, such as Linköping University's rhetoric courses, charge no tuition to citizens of EU/EEA countries and Switzerland. You need to meet the entry requirements and apply in time. Other applicants pay a fee per course. In English, the Open University's Talk the Talk on FutureLearn is described as free, and HarvardX's rhetoric course on edX can be audited free without a certificate.

Are there online rhetoric courses in English?

Yes. Malmö University's Online Presentation Skills for University Studies (AK107E) lists English as the language of instruction. Self-paced courses in English are also available on Coursera, edX and FutureLearn from the University of Washington, Harvard and the Open University.

When can I apply for a spring distance course?

For spring 2027, Linköping University's distance rhetoric courses show that late application opens on 15 December. Check each course page, because rounds and places differ.
