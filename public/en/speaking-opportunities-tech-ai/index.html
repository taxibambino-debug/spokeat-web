Speaking opportunities for tech and AI experts

Written by Ted Scheiman, banking and fintech executive, guest lecturer. Last reviewed 16 November 2026. How we research and fact-check

In tech and AI, many speaking opportunities are published openly. Community meetups publish standing calls for speakers. Developer conferences run public calls for papers. Universities and student societies want people who build real systems to explain how they work.

That openness comes with clear expectations. Some organisers state on their pages that they will not accept sales pitches [8]. The strongest pitches bring a specific problem, an honest account of what worked, and something the audience can use.

This page maps the main venues where tech and AI experts speak, from local meetups to online lectures, with real examples we checked on 16 November 2026. We do not promise that any organiser has a slot for you. We describe where opportunities typically come from and what each type expects.

Key takeaways

  • Meetups are the most open door. Several publish rolling calls, such as AWS User Group Stockholm (open until November 2027) and .NET Skåne (open until October 2027) [8] [9].
  • Online formats remove travel. Voxel51, Data Science Dojo and RMIT Europe all describe virtual talks or lectures [11] [12] [13].
  • Universities and student societies host tech talks, and some explicitly invite contributors [3] [6].
  • Developer conferences run calls months ahead. DevSum's call for 2027 closes on 8 December 2026 [14].
  • No sales pitches. Organisers state this directly [8].

Meetups: the most open door

Tech meetups depend on a steady flow of speakers. Many publish their calls openly and accept first-time speakers. Our guide to getting your first meetup slot covers how to write the proposal.

Examples we verified:

  • AWS User Group Stockholm has a call open from 5 November 2026 to 3 November 2027. It welcomes talks "in any format or at any level of expertise", and states that sales speeches will not be accepted [8].
  • .NET Skåne (Malmö, Lund and Helsingborg region) has a call open until 1 October 2027 for 20 or 45 minute talks in English or Swedish. Topics include AI, cloud and real project experience [9].
  • Stockholm MLOps Community describes itself as "open to everyone", with 5,868 members on its group page. Meetups listed on its group page featured speakers from companies including Ericsson, King and Netlight [7].
  • Stockholm AI, ML & Data Developers Group says "If you'd like to speak at future meetups" and invites people to contact the organisers. Its listed events run online [10].

What meetups want: practical lessons, code or architecture, mistakes and fixes. A talk titled "What broke when we moved our model to production" will usually beat "The future of AI". That is our editorial view, not a published rule.

Online talks, webinars and online guest lectures

Online formats suit busy professionals and people outside big cities. Our guide on guest teaching online explains how online guest lectures differ from campus ones.

Examples we verified:

  • Voxel51 runs AI, ML and computer vision meetups in more than 50 cities, with virtual presentations on Zoom. Talks are 30 minutes plus Q&A, and the page lists what a proposal should include [11].
  • Data Science Dojo hosts one-hour webinars with presentations of about 30 to 45 minutes, streamed live and recorded. Speakers are not paid; the events are a free community service [12].
  • RMIT Europe recruits industry professionals for online guest lectures. Formats range from "a 15 minute Q&A with students" to "a 30 minute presentation, pre-recorded or live" [13].

Universities, research networks and student societies

Universities want practitioners who can connect theory to systems in production. Some publish routes; others work through initiatives and student groups.

Examples we verified:

  • LTH, Lund University's Faculty of Engineering, says its courses "welcome guest lecturers", provided you first build a relationship with the teacher responsible for the course [1].
  • AI Lund, Lund University's open AI network, lists "Share your real world experience as a guest speaker" as a way for industry to take part [6].
  • Stockholm School of Economics' Tech Initiative organises "talks with entrepreneurs, executives, and thought leaders" [2]. Its series "Technology in an uncertain world" says: "If you would be interested in contributing to the series in the future, we would be very happy to hear from you." [3] Past tech talk speakers include leaders from Spotify, Google, Microsoft Sweden and IBM Sweden [4].
  • KTH AI Society runs educational events from introductory machine learning to current research, plus workshops and hackathons with companies [5].

Course types to look for: software engineering, machine learning, data science, information systems, human-computer interaction, cybersecurity, and business courses on digital transformation.

Chambers, associations and innovation hubs

AI is now a standard topic at business breakfasts. These audiences are managers and decision-makers, so pitch impact and lessons rather than code.

Examples we verified:

  • Dutch Chamber of Commerce in Sweden hosted an "interactive Early Breakfast seminar on AI" in May 2025, on what large language models can and cannot do for productivity [16].
  • AmCham Sweden lists a fireside chat on 19 January 2027, co-hosted with BSCC, on moving from AI experiments to AI-driven operations [17] [18]. BSCC also listed a Technology & AI roundtable in October 2026 [18].
  • Ideon Science Park in Lund listed "The Tech Hub Breakfast – HØIBERG IP Strategy in the AI Age" on 19 November [19].

Developer conferences

Conferences give reach, but calls close early. Watch for them months before the event.

  • DevSum (Stockholm, 27 to 28 May 2027) has a call that closes on 8 December 2026. "All sessions are held in English." Travel and accommodation are covered, and the event is free for speakers [14].
  • Øredev (Malmö, 4 to 6 November 2026) closed its call on 31 March 2026. Tracks included Applied AI & Data and Leadership, and travel and accommodation were covered [15].

Topics that tend to work

These are illustrative examples, not guaranteed formats:

  • A post-mortem of a real AI or data project, including what you would change.
  • How a team evaluates, monitors or governs AI models in production.
  • Cost, latency or security trade-offs with numbers from your own work.
  • For students: what an ML engineer, data engineer or AI product manager actually does.
  • For business audiences: how a company moved from pilot to daily use, and what it learned.

How to pitch as a tech or AI expert

  1. Pick the format first. Meetup, course, webinar or breakfast seminar each need a different talk.
  2. Write a specific title. Name the tool, problem or result.
  3. Include a short abstract and bio. Voxel51, for example, asks for a 4 to 5 sentence abstract and a 2 to 3 sentence bio [11].
  4. Leave the product out. Organisers reject sales talks [8].
  5. Follow up once. If there is no reply, move on.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get my first tech talk?

Start with a meetup that has an open call. AWS User Group Stockholm accepts talks "at any level of expertise", and .NET Skåne accepts 20-minute talks [8] [9].

Can I give an AI talk online?

Yes. Voxel51 runs virtual meetups on Zoom, Data Science Dojo runs webinars, and RMIT Europe offers online guest lectures [11] [12] [13].

Do tech conferences pay speakers?

The pages we checked did not mention speaker fees, but some events cover costs. DevSum covers travel and accommodation and charges speakers nothing [14]. Data Science Dojo states speakers are not paid [12].

Can I talk about my company's product?

Not as a pitch. Organisers such as AWS User Group Stockholm will not accept sales speeches [8]. You can describe what you built and learned, as long as the audience gets value without buying anything.
