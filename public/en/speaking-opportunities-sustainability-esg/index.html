Speaking opportunities for sustainability and ESG experts

Written by Ted Scheiman, banking and fintech executive, guest lecturer. Last reviewed 17 November 2026. How we research and fact-check

Sustainability and ESG professionals do work that students read about but rarely see up close. Reporting rules, supply chain audits, climate targets and investor questions all look different from inside a company. That practical view is what many course coordinators, student groups and networks want.

The difficulty is that sustainability is spread across every faculty and every industry. A relevant course might sit in engineering, environmental science, business or a student-led centre. A relevant network might belong to a chamber of commerce, a consultancy or a business association.

This page shows where sustainability and ESG experts speak, with real examples we checked on 17 November 2026. We do not promise that any organiser has a slot for you. We show where opportunities come from and what each type of venue expects.

Key takeaways

  • University courses in sustainable development invite people who work on sustainability in different roles, and some say so in the syllabus [1].
  • Student-led sustainability groups exist at large Swedish universities. Uppsala's CEMUS runs courses and open events with guest lecturers [2] [3].
  • Business networks for sustainability leaders meet several times a year, and some ask members to lead sessions [10].
  • Sustainability conferences mix researchers, executives and policymakers. Large events such as World Water Week take session proposals from organisations, not individuals [16] [17].
  • Media podcasts on ESG interview practitioners such as heads of sustainability [19].

University courses and programmes

Sustainability courses need examples from practice. A lecture on life cycle assessment is more convincing when someone explains how a company actually used one to change a product.

Examples we verified:

  • Chalmers University of Technology, course FFR161 "Hållbar utveckling" (Sustainable development, 7.5 credits). The syllabus says guest lecturers talk about their own experience of working for sustainable development in different roles in society [1].
  • CEMUS, Uppsala University, a student-initiated, interdisciplinary centre where students help shape and coordinate courses together with course coordinators, teachers and researchers. It states that "Researchers are engaged as guest lectures" [2]. Its outreach arm, CEMUS Forum, runs lectures, workshops and seminars that are open to everyone, and invites readers to suggest "an inspiring guest" for its lecture series [3].
  • IIIEE (International Institute for Industrial Environmental Economics), Lund University. In May 2025 the institute reported that Systembolaget's sustainability manager met students on its Environmental Management and Policy master's programme to discuss supply chain work and her own career path [4].

Course types to look for: sustainable development, environmental management, corporate sustainability, sustainable finance, life cycle assessment, circular economy, energy systems and supply chain management. Our guide on finding the right course shows how to read a syllabus for clues like these.

Student associations

Sustainability student groups are often project-driven. They run theme weeks, campaigns and lectures, and they change committees every year. Read our guide on speaking at student societies for how they plan and whom to contact.

Examples we verified:

  • KTH, which lists several student organisations working on environment, climate and sustainability. They include THS Sustainability, KTH Students for Sustainability, Klimatstudenterna and Engineers Without Borders KTH [5].
  • KTH Students for Sustainability (SforS), which "promotes sustainability on KTH's campus" through projects and initiatives and lists a public contact address [6].
  • SASSE Sustainability Group (SSG) at the Stockholm School of Economics. In 2025 SSE described it as one of the largest initiatives in the student association, with more than 35 active members across four sub-projects and two annual week-long events [7].
  • Chalmers Students for Sustainability (CSS), whose site describes lunch lectures as "a crash course on different sustainable challenges and solutions" with guest lecturers from industry and academia. When we checked, the page still said no lunch lectures were planned, so treat this as a past format and ask before you pitch [8].

What they usually want: a clear explanation of a real problem, honest trade-offs, and career insight. They are sceptical of polished corporate messages.

Business networks and associations

Sustainability networks bring together people with the same job in different companies. That makes them a good place for peer talks. Our guide on speaking at industry associations and chambers explains how these programmes are planned.

Examples we verified:

  • CSR Sweden, a network of companies from different sectors. Its Knowledge Hubs cover topics such as ESG and reporting, human rights and due diligence, and responsible taxation. The hubs produce seminars and training, and are often invited to speak at conferences [9].
  • Handelskammarens Hållbarhetsnätverk (Sydsvenska Industri- och Handelskammaren), a network for sustainability leaders. Its information sheet from 2022 lists about ten events a year in Malmö, Växjö and Helsingborg, and invites members to help lead lectures, workshops and roundtables [10].
  • WSP's environment and sustainability network in Örebro, a breakfast network with meetings at WSP's office. Each meeting opens with a legislation update and a theme introduction, followed by discussion. Its page listed four meetings in 2025 [11].

A note on networks run by companies: some are run by consultancies or other firms. That is fine, but ask who sets the agenda and whether guest speakers are part of the format.

Conferences and podcasts

Sustainability conferences vary widely. Some are academic forums, some are business summits and some are huge international weeks where organisations host their own sessions.

  • HoSS Forum 2026 at the Stockholm School of Economics took place on 16 October 2026. The panel included the CFO of Alfa Laval and SSAB's VP Sustainability alongside researchers and a Member of the European Parliament [12]. The annual forum ran as the Misum Forum from 2020 to 2025 [13].
  • Gothenburg Sustainability Summit 2026 took place on 21 April 2026, created by the WIN WIN Gothenburg Sustainability Award and CSR Västsverige. It was held in Swedish with some sessions in English, and its speakers included researchers, public officials and SKF's Director of Public Affairs [14].
  • Lund Sustainability Week, a joint initiative of Lund University and Lund Municipality, ran its 10th edition from 13 to 17 April 2026 with over 70 events. The IIIEE described the programme as featuring "experts, practitioners and students" [15].
  • World Water Week in Stockholm is organised by SIWI. The 2026 edition drew participants from more than 180 countries, and SIWI has announced the 2027 theme "Water for Human and Planetary Health" [16]. Sessions are hosted by convening organisations. For 2026, the on-site session deadline was 23 February 2026 [17]. We found no published 2027 call when we checked.
  • A Sustainable Tomorrow 2026 on 10 September 2026 had a Stockholm hub hosted by TechSverige and Telia, following a live broadcast with speakers from research, business and the public sector [18].
  • ESG-podden, Realtid's ESG podcast, has interviewed people such as Skanska's EVP for sustainability and innovation and the secretary general of the AP funds' ethics council. When we checked, it was preparing season four [19].

Topics that tend to work

These are illustrative examples, not guaranteed formats:

  • How a reporting requirement changed daily work in a sustainability or finance team.
  • What a supply chain audit looks like in practice, as in the IIIEE example [4].
  • How a company set, measured and missed (or met) a climate target.
  • Trade-offs between cost, timing and impact in a real project, without confidential details.
  • Career paths into sustainability roles, for students choosing a direction.

Experts in adjacent fields can find more angles in our pages on finance and fintech, tech and AI and marketing and communications.

How to pitch as a sustainability expert

  1. Avoid greenwashing in your own pitch. Organisers and students notice claims without evidence. Use numbers you can stand behind.
  2. Name one topic. "Sustainability" is a field. "What we learned from our first double materiality assessment" is a talk.
  3. Show credibility briefly. Your role, your years in the field and one concrete result.
  4. Keep it non-commercial. Student and community organisers rarely accept sales talks.
  5. Follow up once. If there is no reply, move on to the next organiser.

Frequently asked questions

Where can a sustainability expert give a guest lecture?

Look for courses in sustainable development, environmental management and corporate sustainability. Chalmers' FFR161 syllabus explicitly includes guest lecturers who work on sustainable development [1], and Uppsala's CEMUS runs open lectures and invites guest suggestions [3].

How do I get a session at World Water Week?

Sessions are proposed by convening organisations through SIWI's call for sessions. For 2026, deadlines ran from 23 February to 19 March 2026 depending on format [17]. If you work for an organisation, ask whether it plans to convene a session.

Do sustainability student groups invite outside speakers?

Many run lectures and theme weeks. SASSE Sustainability Group runs two week-long events a year [7], and Chalmers Students for Sustainability has run lunch lectures with industry guests [8]. Contact the committee directly.

Is a sustainability network a good place for a first talk?

Often, yes. Peer networks are small and focused, and some ask members to lead sessions. Handelskammaren's sustainability network invites members to help lead lectures and workshops [10].
