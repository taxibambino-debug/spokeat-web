From first talk to keynote: a 12-month speaking plan

Written by Ted Scheiman, banking and fintech executive, guest lecturer. Last reviewed 16 November 2026. How we research and fact-check

Many professionals want to give a keynote one day. Few have a plan for getting there. They wait for an invitation, or they send one application to a big conference and hear nothing back.

A better approach is a ladder. Each rung gives you practice, a reference and a reason for the next organiser to say yes. This plan covers twelve months: meetup, student society, university course, association and conference. It is realistic about what a year can deliver, and it is built around how organisers actually plan their programmes.

One honest point up front: a year is enough to become a credible conference speaker. Whether someone invites you to open their event as the keynote depends on your field, your topic and luck. This plan puts you in a position to be asked.

Key takeaways

  • Climb in order: meetup, society, course, association, conference. Each step gives you proof for the next.
  • Conferences plan far ahead. brightonSEO starts line-up planning about six months before each event [1], and several 2027 calls for speakers close in November and December 2026.
  • Start practising before you pitch. Low-stakes formats such as meetups accept speakers at any experience level [2].
  • University teaching runs in terms and study periods, so pitch a guest lecture well before the course starts [3].
  • "Keynote" in the paid sense is a different market. Agencies look for speakers already earning substantial fees [4].

The plan at a glance

Months Rung Goal Proof you leave with
1–2 Foundation One topic, one talk outline, a speaker profile, regular practice A tested 20-minute talk
2–4 Meetup First external talk to a friendly audience A first reference and, ideally, a recording
3–6 Student society A talk or panel for students A second audience type
4–8 University course A guest lecture tied to a specific course A credible line in your bio
6–9 Association or chamber A seminar or panel for peers Visibility among decision-makers
6–12 Conference Applications to calls for speakers A conference talk, or a strong application for next year

The months overlap on purpose. Conference deadlines come early, so you may apply to a conference while you are still giving meetup talks.

Months 1–2: build the foundation

Pick one topic

Choose one area where you have first-hand experience and something specific to say. A narrow topic beats a broad one. brightonSEO explicitly asks for specific, narrow topics rather than broad areas like "social media marketing" [1].

Write a 20-minute version

Twenty minutes is close to many talk slots. brightonSEO's main-track talks are 20 minutes [1], and Nordic Tech Days gives speakers 24 minutes plus questions [14]. A short version is easier to place and easier to extend later.

Set up your profile

Write a short bio, choose a photo and list two talk titles. See what organisers check in a speaker profile.

Practise regularly

If you have no outlet for practice at work, consider a speaking club. Toastmasters International, for example, says members practise at every meeting through prepared speeches, impromptu speaking or meeting roles, and that giving and receiving feedback is "an integral part" of the programme [5]. Any setting where you speak regularly and get honest feedback will do.

Months 2–4: your first meetup talk

Meetups are the lowest-risk stage. Audiences are small, the tone is informal and organisers often need speakers. AWS User Group Stockholm says "Speakers are always welcome!" and accepts talks from beginner to expert level [2]. ProductTank Stockholm's events include talks from guest speakers on product management topics [6].

What to do: - Find two or three meetups in your field that have held events in the past few months. - Look for a speaker form or a "call for proposals" link. If there is none, message the organiser through the platform. - Offer your 20-minute talk with a clear title and one-line takeaway. - Ask if the session will be recorded. A clip is useful later.

Our guide to getting your first meetup slot covers this step in detail.

Months 3–6: a student society

Student societies run their own speaker events, and they are often keen on practitioners. At the Stockholm School of Economics, the student Entrepreneurship Society runs Initiate, "a series of inspirational lectures by entrepreneurs" [7]. Societies also run panels and fireside chats, which are a good way to practise answering questions live.

Students are a different audience from peers. They want to know what your job is actually like. In one survey of 114 undergraduates, students valued industry speakers who shared insider information about their day-to-day work and gave career advice [8]. Adapt your talk accordingly. See how to speak at student societies.

Months 4–8: a guest lecture in a course

A guest lecture in a university course is a strong credential. It shows that an academic has judged your expertise relevant to their teaching.

Time it to the academic calendar

Courses are planned per term and often per study period. At KTH, for example, the 2027/28 academic year has four study periods, with the first running from 30 August to 15 October 2027 [3]. Contact the course coordinator well before the period in which the course runs. Pitching the week a course starts rarely works.

Use the routes universities publish

Many universities publish a route for practitioners. Stockholm University's alumni office says guest lecturers with real-world experience are "in high demand" and offers to match alumni with a suitable audience [9]. Sheffield University Management School describes sessions of 30 to 40 minutes plus 15 to 20 minutes of questions [10], which shows that a guest slot is often close to an hour.

Read the practical guide to getting speaking opportunities for how to find the right course and contact.

Months 6–9: an association or chamber of commerce

Associations and chambers of commerce put you in front of peers, clients and decision-makers. Their formats are usually breakfast seminars, panels and fireside chats. The British-Swedish Chamber of Commerce, for instance, lists breakfast seminars and after-work seminars among its activities [11].

Panels are a realistic way in. Organisers need several voices per panel, and a panel slot usually needs less preparation than a solo talk. Use your course and meetup experience as proof that you can handle a room.

Months 6–12: conferences

Watch the deadlines, not the event dates

Conferences select speakers months ahead. Some examples for 2027 events, checked on 16 November 2026:

Event Event date Call closes Notes
DevSum, Stockholm 27–28 May 2027 8 December 2026 Travel and accommodation covered for speakers [12]
Money20/20 Europe, Amsterdam 8–10 June 2027 27 November 2026 Selected speakers are offered a discounted pass at €2,495 + VAT [13]
Nordic Tech Days, Tallinn 2–3 June 2027 31 December 2026 Travel up to €350 and two hotel nights covered [14]

The pattern is clear: if you want to speak in May or June, you apply around November or December of the year before. Read the terms carefully. Some conferences cover speakers' travel; some expect speakers to buy a pass. Use our guide to calls for speakers for how to write a proposal.

Expect rejection, and reuse your work

Large calls are competitive. brightonSEO says unsuccessful pitches usually get no reply and may be rolled forward to future events [1]. Money20/20 Europe says regular speakers who appear at more than two events a year may be asked to make room for new voices [13], which works in a newcomer's favour.

Month 12: what "keynote" really means

There are two kinds of keynote. One is the opening or closing talk at an event, where organisers invite someone they trust to set the tone. The other is the paid keynote market run by speaker agencies. These are different games.

For the paid market, the bar is high. BigSpeak, a US speakers bureau, says speakers it lists should already be earning around $10,000 or more per engagement, and it typically takes a 25–35% commission [4]. That is not where most professionals start, and it is not required for visibility or credibility.

For the first kind, the path is the one in this plan. Organisers can only invite people they know about. A year of meetups, societies, courses and associations puts you in front of far more of them.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to become a keynote speaker?

There is no fixed timeline. A year of steady talks can make you a credible conference speaker in your field. Keynote invitations depend on your topic, reputation and network, and paid keynote work is a separate market with much higher entry requirements [4].

Do I need to join a speaking club?

No, but you need regular practice with feedback. Clubs such as Toastmasters offer a structured way to get it [5]. Internal presentations at work and meetup talks also count.

When should I apply to conferences for next year?

Earlier than you think. Several 2027 conferences close their calls in November or December 2026 [12] [13] [14]. Check each event's call-for-speakers page and put the deadlines in your calendar.

Can I skip straight to conferences?

You can apply, and some conferences welcome first-time speakers [1]. But a few smaller talks give you a tested talk, references and possibly a recording, which make your application much stronger.
