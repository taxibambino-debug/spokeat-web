Do guest lecturers get paid? Fees, expenses and what to agree in writing

Written by Ted Scheiman, banking and fintech executive, guest lecturer. Last reviewed 17 November 2026. How we research and fact-check

You have been asked to give a guest lecture, or you are about to ask for one. Then the awkward question comes up: is there a fee, who pays for the train, and do you need a contract for a 90-minute session?

The honest answer is that it varies a lot. Some universities pay nothing beyond a thank you. Others publish fixed rates. This guide shows real, published examples with the date we checked them, explains how expenses usually work, covers how payment works in Sweden, and gives you a short list of things to agree in writing.

This is general information, not tax or legal advice. Rules change, and your situation may differ. Check with Skatteverket (or your local tax authority), your employer or an accountant before you invoice.

Key takeaways

  • Many guest lectures are unpaid or paid with a small honorarium. Some institutions publish fixed rates, and these are often modest.
  • Travel expenses are often reimbursed even when there is no fee, but only if you agree it in advance and keep receipts.
  • In Sweden, you are paid either as an individual (the payer deducts tax) or by invoice with F-skatt. Skatteverket explains both routes.
  • If you are employed, check your employer's rules on outside work before you accept a fee.
  • Agree date, format, recording, materials, expenses, payment and cancellation in a short email before the day.

What is normal? Published examples

Universities rarely publish what they pay guest speakers. The ones that do show a wide range. These examples are not a market rate. They show what a few institutions have written down.

Institution What it publishes Checked
Boston University Metropolitan College (USA) Honoraria for guest speakers of $50 for a one-hour lecture, $100 for two hours and $150 for three [1] 17 Nov 2026
Lindenwood University (USA) Class speaker honorarium "typically" about $50, which may be a payment, lunch with the instructor or university merchandise [2] 17 Nov 2026
Whitman College (USA) Standard honorarium of $500 for visiting educators on campus; $150–200 recommended for a 50-minute virtual session [3] 17 Nov 2026
University of Sussex, School of Media, Arts and Humanities (UK) Research visitors: flat £200 for self-employed or freelance speakers; salaried academics are not normally offered a fee, but expenses are reimbursed [4] 17 Nov 2026
Uppsala Senioruniversitet (Sweden) Autumn 2026: SEK 4,000 per lecture, SEK 4,600 if filmed, SEK 5,200 if filmed and the film is sold to another senior university [5] 17 Nov 2026
Örebro University, School of Medical Sciences (Sweden) 2026 hourly rates for external teaching by role, for example SEK 400 per hour for a senior lecturer without a medical licence and SEK 876 for a professor with one [6] 17 Nov 2026

Two patterns stand out. First, many course slots pay little or nothing. A guest lecture is mainly a chance to reach students and build credibility, not an income stream. Second, salaried professionals are sometimes treated differently from freelancers. Sussex, for example, does not normally pay a fee to visitors who are salaried elsewhere [4].

Some institutions also limit who can be paid. The University of Rochester's 2026 policy says its senior leaders may not receive extra pay for guest lecturing in the university's own courses without prior written approval [7].

If the opportunity is unpaid, that is normal, and it is not the same as being asked to pay. Our guide to paying to speak covers when money flows the other way.

Expenses: what is usually covered

Even without a fee, many institutions reimburse reasonable travel. Sussex reimburses travel, accommodation and subsistence for visiting speakers through an expense claim form with receipts [4]. Whitman College says airfare for visiting educators should follow the same guidelines as for its employees, and it reimburses use of a personal car at the IRS standard mileage rate [3]. Uppsala Senioruniversitet pays SEK 25 per Swedish mil (10 km) for driving outside the local bus area and asks for tickets for train or air travel [5].

What this means in practice:

  • Ask before you book whether travel is covered, and which class of ticket.
  • Ask whether the organiser books the hotel or you do.
  • Keep every receipt and claim quickly. Uppsala Senioruniversitet asks for claims within two weeks of the lecture [5].
  • For an online lecture, expenses rarely apply, so the fee question is the only one.

How payment works in Sweden

A Swedish university or organisation can pay you in two main ways. Which one applies affects tax and paperwork.

Paid as an individual (no F-skatt)

If you do not have F-skatt, the payer normally deducts tax from what it pays you for work and also pays employer contributions (arbetsgivaravgifter), according to Skatteverket [8]. For one-off payments or side income that is not your main income, Skatteverket says the payer deducts 30 per cent tax [9]. Umeå University's rule on external teachers describes the same route: an individual is paid a fee that is taxed, and the university pays social fees [10].

Invoicing with F-skatt

If you are approved for F-skatt, Skatteverket says the client should not deduct tax or pay employer contributions on what it pays you [8]. You pay your own preliminary tax and contributions instead [11]. If you are employed and also run a sole trader business, you can hold FA-skatt. Then you must refer to your F-skatt approval in writing for each assignment, or the payer treats you as A-skatt [8].

Not every side income is a business. Skatteverket uses three criteria for business activity: independence, a profit motive and continuity [12]. A single lecture may not meet them. You apply for F-skatt through the e-service at verksamt.se [13].

Some organisers set their own invoice requirements. Uppsala Senioruniversitet, for example, asks that invoices show the fee, VAT, your organisation number and a statement that you are approved for F-skatt [5].

Through an umbrella company (egenanställning)

A third option is an umbrella company. Skatteverket describes it this way: the company has F-skatt, invoices the client for a commission, reports employer contributions, deducts tax and pays you the rest as salary [14]. Skatteverket also notes you cannot have the company invoice for work you do as an employee of another employer [14].

If you are employed

If you lecture about your day job, the work and the fee may fall under your employer's rules. Read speaking while employed: employer approval and outside-activity rules before you accept money.

What to agree in writing

You do not need a long contract for one session. A short email that both sides confirm is usually enough. Some institutions require more: Umeå University says agreements to use companies in teaching must be in writing and approved in advance [10].

Cover these points:

Topic What to confirm
Date and time Date, start and end time, time zone if online
Format Lecture, seminar or workshop; group size; level of students
Topic and fit The course or programme, and what the teacher wants students to learn
Fee Amount, whether VAT is added, and how you will be paid (payroll or invoice)
Expenses What is covered, who books, and how to claim
Recording Whether the session is filmed or streamed, where it is published and for how long. Recording can change the fee, as the Uppsala Senioruniversitet rates show [5]
Materials Whether slides are shared with students and on which platform
Cancellation What happens if either side cancels, and whether expenses already paid are refunded

Ask early, and ask once. A polite question such as "Is there an honorarium or expense budget for guest speakers?" is normal. If the answer is no, decide whether the audience is worth it to you.

Frequently asked questions

Do guest lecturers get paid?

Sometimes. Many university guest lectures are unpaid or carry a small honorarium, and some institutions publish fixed rates. Boston University Metropolitan College publishes $50 per lecture hour, while Whitman College publishes $500 for on-campus visits. Always ask before you agree.

Should I ask for a guest lecture fee?

It is fine to ask whether there is an honorarium or expense budget. Ask once, early and politely. Many courses have no budget, so be ready to decide whether the audience is worth your time without a fee.

Do I need F-skatt to be paid for a lecture in Sweden?

No. Without F-skatt, the payer normally deducts tax and pays employer contributions. With F-skatt, you invoice and handle tax yourself. Skatteverket explains both, and an umbrella company is a third route. Check your own case with Skatteverket or an accountant.

Are travel expenses covered for guest speakers?

Often, but not always. Many institutions reimburse reasonable travel and accommodation against receipts. Agree what is covered before you book.
