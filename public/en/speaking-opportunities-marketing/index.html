Speaking opportunities for marketing and communications professionals

Written by Ted Scheiman, banking and fintech executive, guest lecturer. Last reviewed 16 November 2026. How we research and fact-check

Marketers and communicators speak for a living, in a way. You pitch ideas, present results and explain brands. Yet many marketing professionals never take that skill onto a stage, a lecture hall or a webinar.

The opportunities are there, but they are spread across very different places: communication schools that bring in practitioners, business school courses, creative industry networks, specialist conferences and brand webinars. Each has its own gatekeeper and its own idea of a good talk.

This page shows where marketing and communications professionals typically speak, with real examples we checked on 16 November 2026. We do not promise that any of these organisers has a slot for you. We describe where opportunities usually come from and what each one expects.

Key takeaways

  • Communication schools invite working practitioners. Berghs School of Communication in Stockholm publicly invites guest lecturers to get in touch [1].
  • University courses on media, creative industries and management use outside speakers, including on personal branding and marketing [2] [3] [4].
  • Industry networks such as Media Evolution in Malmö run seminars with practitioner speakers [7] [8].
  • Specialist conferences want narrow, practical talks. brightonSEO's main track talks last twenty minutes and pitches are rolling [10].
  • Brand webinars offer reach in exchange for expertise, but read what you are agreeing to [11].

Communication schools

Communication schools train people for agencies, in-house teams and creative roles. Practitioner input is part of what they offer.

Example we verified:

  • Berghs School of Communication (Stockholm) writes that it brings in "guest lecturers and professionals from around the world" to give students industry insight beyond Sweden. Its 2025 article ends: "Passing through Sweden and interested in delivering a guest lecture to Berghs students? Don't hesitate to reach out!" [1]

What to pitch: a real campaign, a craft skill or a hard lesson, explained so that students can apply it in their own projects.

University courses

Media, communication, design and business courses often connect students with working professionals. The course coordinator decides. Our guide on finding the right course for a guest lecture shows how to read a syllabus and spot a fit.

Examples we verified:

  • Malmö University offers a master's course, "Media and Communication Studies: Understanding the Creative Industries", where "Students will meet local and/or international creative and cultural actors" who discuss their work [2].
  • Lund University School of Economics and Management (LUSEM) reported a guest lecture in which a course director invited two London-based designers to speak in a Human-Computer Interaction design course. Their book was the course's main literature [6].
  • Sheffield University Management School lists past guest talks including "Personal branding expertise" on a professional self-management module [3].

Course types to look for: marketing management, consumer behaviour, digital marketing, brand management, strategic communication, media and creative industries, and design.

Business schools and practitioner programmes

Business schools invite practitioners into management programmes, and marketing is often named explicitly.

Examples we verified:

  • Cambridge Judge Business School invites people to "become a Practitioner Insight Speaker, sharing your experiences in specific business areas such as marketing or finance". Its students also seek corporate speakers for club events [4].
  • Jönköping International Business School (JIBS) says "someone from your company/organisation can come and hold a guest lecture/speech at JIBS" [5].

Industry networks and associations

Industry networks are where peers meet. The audience is other professionals, so the bar for originality is higher than in a classroom. Our guide on speaking at industry associations and chambers covers how these programmes are planned.

Example we verified:

  • Media Evolution in Malmö runs a programme of classes, seminars and social gatherings [7]. In September 2025 it hosted a free seminar, "Rethinking Social Media Platforms – New Strategies for Engaging an Audience", with speakers including a digital rights analyst and a researcher on internet culture [8]. A June 2026 seminar gave each speaker a 10-minute slot to share a change, a signal and a hoped-for future [9].

Short slots are a good start. A 10-minute slot in a shared programme is less daunting than a full keynote and still puts you in front of peers.

Specialist conferences

Marketing also has specialist conferences, for example in search. They tend to expect deep, practical expertise.

Example we verified:

  • brightonSEO accepts speaker pitches on a rolling basis for brightonSEO, Hero Conf and MeasureFest. It wants "fresh voices alongside well-known names" and practical talks. It asks you not to pitch self-promotional content such as tool demos or client showcases. "Our main track talks last twenty minutes." Speakers receive a two-day all-access ticket, and the team says it typically starts planning line-ups about six months before each event [10].

What this tells you: narrow topics win. "How we fixed crawl budget on a 2-million-page site" is a stronger pitch than "SEO trends". That example is hypothetical.

Webinars and online events

Many marketing software companies run webinars with outside experts. These give reach quickly, but the host's commercial interest is part of the deal.

Example we verified:

  • Sendible, a social media management company, says it is "always looking for inspiring speakers, educators, and hosts to co-lead webinars, panel discussions, and live events". It offers exposure to marketers and business owners, co-marketing support and access to future speaking opportunities in its network [11].

Before you agree: check whether you will be presenting your own expertise or endorsing the host's product. Keep your talk useful to people who will never buy it.

Topics that tend to work

These are illustrative examples, not guaranteed formats:

  • A campaign breakdown with goals, numbers and what you would change.
  • How a team measures marketing impact when tracking is limited.
  • Practical SEO, content or social media methods tested on real accounts.
  • Crisis or change communication, told as a process rather than a war story.
  • For students: what a brand manager, communications lead or growth marketer does all week.

How to pitch as a marketer or communicator

  1. Pick one audience. Students, peers and business owners need different talks.
  2. Lead with the takeaway. brightonSEO asks for a title, description, ideal audience and what attendees will take away [10].
  3. Prove it with work, not adjectives. One campaign, one number, one lesson.
  4. Stay non-promotional. Organisers reject tool demos and client showcases [10].
  5. Follow up once. If there is no reply, move on.

Frequently asked questions

Can I give a guest lecture at a communication school?

Yes, some invite it openly. Berghs School of Communication publishes an invitation to guest lecturers to get in touch [1]. Contact the programme or course lead with a specific topic.

How do I get a talk at brightonSEO?

Submit a pitch through its speaker pitch form, one idea per pitch, with a clear title, audience and takeaways. Only successful speakers hear back, and unsuccessful pitches may roll forward to future events [10].

Are marketing webinars worth doing?

They can be, if the audience fits and you keep the content educational. Sendible, for example, offers co-marketing support and exposure to its audience [11]. Make sure you are not just endorsing the host's product.

Which university courses invite marketing practitioners?

Look at marketing, communication, media and creative industries courses. Malmö University's creative industries master's course and Sheffield's professional self-management module are two examples that describe outside speakers [2] [3].
