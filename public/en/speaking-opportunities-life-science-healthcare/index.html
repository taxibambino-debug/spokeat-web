Speaking opportunities for life science and healthcare experts

Written by Ted Scheiman, banking and fintech executive, guest lecturer. Last reviewed 17 November 2026. How we research and fact-check

People in biotech, pharma, medtech and healthcare know how ideas become products and how products reach patients. Students rarely see that path. Researchers often want to understand it. Peers in other companies want to hear what worked and what failed.

Life science speaking slots are spread across medical universities, engineering schools, student career groups, regional clusters and large conferences. Some formats are open to anyone with a good proposal. Others are closed to commercial content or tied to membership.

This page shows where life science and healthcare experts speak, with real examples we checked on 17 November 2026. It is about speaking opportunities, not about health or treatment. We do not promise that any organiser has a slot for you.

Key takeaways

  • Medical and life science courses invite practitioners. Karolinska Institutet's biotechnology business course says students meet practitioners from finance, law, politics and corporate strategy [1].
  • Student organisations at KI connect students with the life science industry through career events, a TEDx event and professional workshops [5].
  • Regional clusters such as Medicon Valley Alliance run seminars, member meetings and topic networks every month [9].
  • Vitalis 2027 in Gothenburg accepts presentation proposals until 15 December 2026. Content must be current, referenced and non-commercial, and speakers pay their own travel [12].
  • Life science podcasts interview company leaders and investors [14] [15].

University courses and programmes

Life science courses teach the science well. What students miss is how a company decides which project to fund, how a clinical study is set up in practice, or how a device gets through procurement.

Examples we verified:

  • Karolinska Institutet, course 2QA214 "From Science to Business: Concepts in Biotechnology" (7.5 credits). The syllabus says "students meet practitioners and researchers with a Swedish and international background". Practitioners "present specific experiences from starting and running knowledge intensive young businesses" [1].
  • Karolinska Institutet's Master's Programme in Bioentrepreneurship, which says "many of the lecturers on the programme are experienced researchers or life science business leaders" [2].
  • University of Gothenburg, course BIO523 "Utveckling av läkemedel" (Drug development, 7.5 credits). The syllabus says invited lecturers will cover applied and basic-science aspects, and one learning outcome is knowledge of practical working methods used in industry [3].
  • Lund University (LTH), course EEMA01 Biomedical Design. One learning outcome is to "discuss medical device design with medtech companies and healthcare professionals" [4]. The syllabus does not mention guest lectures, so ask the course coordinator what form industry contact takes.

Course types to look for: drug development, bioentrepreneurship, medical technology, biomedical engineering, health economics, regulatory affairs, clinical research and health informatics. Planning matters, because coordinators often fix the schedule a term in advance.

Student organisations

Medical and life science students are often unsure what jobs exist outside research and clinical work. That makes career-focused student groups a natural fit for industry speakers. Our guide on speaking at student societies covers how they plan.

Examples we verified:

  • Medicinska Föreningen (MF) at KI, whose business committee "builds connections between students and the life science industry". Its flagship event CHaSE is described as "one of the largest student-led career fairs in Sweden" [5].
  • TEDxKI, an independently run TED event at Aula Medica curated by student volunteers, with a speaker scouting team [5].
  • Synapse, a non-profit that helps students "connect with professionals". Its Stockholm hub is open to all students studying in Stockholm [5]. In Lund, Synapse once ran a career evening at Medicon Village with speakers from Medicon Valley Alliance, Alligator Bioscience, Novo Nordisk and others. That event was in 2017, so treat it as a format example [6].

What they usually want: a clear account of what your job involves, how you got there and what skills matter. They do not want a product pitch.

Clusters, associations and professional societies

Cluster organisations and societies run frequent events for members. Many have topic networks where a practitioner with one specific lesson is welcome. Our guide on speaking at industry associations and chambers explains how these programmes work.

Examples we verified:

  • Medicon Valley Alliance (MVA), which says it has brought people, organisations and ideas together across borders for three decades. Its calendar listed a "Good Morning Meeting" member meeting on 9 December 2026 and an R&D Network meeting at Malmö University on 14 January 2027 titled "Can Nordic Health Data be a Competitive Advantage in Life Science?". The page notes that 2027 is MVA's 30th anniversary year [9].
  • Medtechdagen 2026, a Swedish-language conference for the medical technology sector held on 24 September 2026 at Life City in Stockholm, listed on MVA's calendar as an external event [10].
  • Apotekarsocieteten (Swedish Pharmaceutical Society). Its 5th Swedish Medicinal Chemistry Symposium on 22 and 23 April 2026 invited poster abstracts and proposals for short talks on recent "hot from the lab data" [11].

Conferences and podcasts

Large life science conferences fall into two groups: content conferences with open calls, and partnering events where most of the value is in meetings.

  • Vitalis 2027 takes place on 24 to 27 May 2027 at the Swedish Exhibition and Congress Centre in Gothenburg. The deadline for presentation proposals is 15 December 2026, with notification in March 2027. Industry representatives may submit, but content must not promote their own products. Proposals must be current, relevant, referenced and non-commercial. Accepted lecturers get a conference card, but "lecturers are responsible for travel, hotels and other costs" [12].
  • Nordic Life Science Days (NLSDays) 2026, organised by SwedenBIO, took place on 8 and 9 September 2026 in Stockholm. SwedenBIO expected around 1,500 delegates and listed six supersessions, a pitch competition with 14 companies and a poster session with 25 academic and industry projects [7] [8]. We found no 2027 information when we checked.
  • BioStock MedTech Summit 2027 in Malmö promises short talks from medtech innovators with Q&A. When we checked, the date and speakers had not been announced [13].
  • Health and Wealth, BioStock's Swedish-language podcast about investing in life science, interviews CEOs, founders and investors in long conversations. The latest episode we saw was published on 19 February 2026 [14].
  • SwedenBIO Studion runs themed seasons on life science topics. Its second season, made with CTR-gruppen, features guests from the pharmaceutical industry on early drug development [15].

Before you submit to any open call, read our call for speakers guide.

A note for healthcare professionals

If you work in healthcare, two points matter more than in most fields. First, check your employer's rules before you accept. Vitalis, for example, requires lecturers to have any necessary permission from their employer or client [12]. Second, never use identifiable patient information in a talk. Keep the focus on systems, processes and lessons, not individual cases. If a talk touches on health outcomes, cite published sources, as Vitalis requires for its programme [12].

Topics that tend to work

These are illustrative examples, not guaranteed formats:

  • What happens between a promising lab result and a first clinical study, as in the SwedenBIO Studion season [15].
  • How investors assess an early-stage biotech or medtech company.
  • What procurement and implementation look like when a hospital adopts a new digital tool.
  • Careers outside academic research, for students choosing a path [6].
  • Lessons from a regulatory submission, without confidential details.

Experts who also work with finance or software can find more ideas in our pages on finance and fintech and tech and AI.

How to pitch as a life science expert

  1. Match the audience. Students want careers and context. Researchers want the route to application. Peers want what is new.
  2. Name one topic. "Drug development" is a field. "Three reasons our first study protocol was rewritten" is a talk.
  3. Show credibility briefly. Your role, your years in the field and one concrete result.
  4. Keep it non-commercial. Educational hosts expect teaching, not selling. Vitalis states that presentations must not be used to market commercial products [12].
  5. Follow up once. If there is no reply, move on to the next organiser.

Frequently asked questions

Where can a medtech or biotech expert give a guest lecture?

Look at medical universities and engineering schools with courses in drug development, bioentrepreneurship and medical technology. KI's 2QA214 and GU's BIO523 both use invited lecturers [1] [3].

How do I speak at Vitalis 2027?

Submit a presentation proposal through the online form by 15 December 2026. Proposals must be current, referenced and non-commercial, and speakers cover their own travel and hotel costs [12].

Can I speak at Nordic Life Science Days?

NLSDays is a partnering event organised by SwedenBIO with supersessions, a pitch competition and a poster session [7]. We found no public speaker call for 2027 when we checked, so watch SwedenBIO's channels.

Do life science student groups invite industry speakers?

Yes. At KI, Medicinska Föreningen's business committee and Synapse both connect students with professionals, and TEDxKI scouts speakers [5].
