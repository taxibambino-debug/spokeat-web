Speaking opportunities in value-based pricing

Revenue optimisation and pricing strategies.

Price is one of the strongest levers on profit and one of the areas where companies most often guess. With costs rising and customers more price-conscious, leaders and founders want to hear how others have reasoned. Talks that get booked rest on real pricing decisions: how you moved from hourly rates to packages, how a price rise was communicated without customers walking away, or what you learned when a new pricing model did not work as planned.

Business schools and economics courses invite practitioners who can show what pricing looks like beyond the spreadsheet, and business networks and industry associations run breakfast meetings and seminars. Start-up meetups and webinars for software companies are other common outlets. Organisers expect you to explain the reasoning behind your decisions and to be careful with confidential figures.

Questions

Can I talk about pricing without showing real prices?

Yes. Use relative changes or invented examples and say clearly that they are illustrations.

Which audiences are most interested?

Founders, sales managers and business students. Adapt your examples to whether the audience sells services, products or subscriptions.

Can I show client work in a talk?

Only what you are allowed to share. Ask the client or anonymise the case; organisers care more about the lesson than the logo.
