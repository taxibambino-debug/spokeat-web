Speaking opportunities in franchising and global scaling

Expanding a business concept into new markets.

Taking a working business concept to a new city or country is one of the hardest stages in a company's life. Many founders are weighing franchising or international expansion but have few people to ask who have actually done it. Organisers therefore book speakers with first-hand experience of expansion: how you chose the first market, what could not be copied from home and how you found partners who shared your values and ways of working.

Entrepreneurship programmes and courses in international business invite practitioners, while export networks, chambers of commerce and business associations hold seminars and panels. Entrepreneurship podcasts are another natural outlet. Organisers expect you to speak openly about cultural and practical obstacles and to separate general lessons from what was specific to your own industry.

Questions

Do I need to have expanded into many countries?

No. A single expansion you can describe in detail is often worth more than a long list of markets.

Should I cover the legal side of franchising?

Describe the issues you ran into, but refer the audience to advisers for legal judgements.

Do I need a successful exit to speak?

No. Honest stories about early stages, failures and pivots are often more useful to audiences than success stories.
