Speaking opportunities in ESG and sustainability reporting

Navigating new rules and reporting requirements for companies.

New EU sustainability reporting rules have turned ESG into a matter for the finance function rather than only for communications. Many companies are partway through their first report and want to hear from someone who has already been through the materiality assessment, data collection and assurance. What makes a talk bookable is concrete lessons: how responsibility was split, which data were missing and how leadership was brought on board.

Demand comes from audit and accounting courses, business school programmes in sustainable finance and industry associations running breakfast seminars for member companies. Webinars work well because audiences tend to wrestle with the same problem at the same moment. Organisers want you to stay within your own experience, be clear that rules and timelines can shift, and avoid giving legal advice from the stage.

Questions

Do I need to be an auditor to speak on ESG reporting?

No. Organisers often book the person who led the work internally, provided you say where your knowledge ends and refer technical interpretation to specialists.

How do I keep the talk current when the rules change?

Refresh your examples before each booking and focus on ways of working that hold even when the details are adjusted.

What do sustainability organisers want from practitioners?

Concrete experience of delivery: data, trade-offs, costs and results, including what did not work.
