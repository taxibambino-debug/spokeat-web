Speaking opportunities in practical rhetoric

Communicating so that people listen and are persuaded.

Speaking so that others listen and take the message on board is a skill in demand in almost every profession, from engineers explaining a project to managers bringing an organisation with them. Audiences want more than the history of rhetoric. Talks that get booked come from people who have faced difficult audiences themselves: a negotiation that turned, a message that landed badly the first time, or a presentation that got a decision through.

University courses, student societies preparing for case competitions and pitches, professional networks and industry days book the subject, often as a workshop with exercises. Webinars work for shorter sessions. Organisers expect you to be a clear speaker yourself, to draw on real situations and to pitch the level to whether the audience is new to speaking or experienced.

Questions

Do I need formal training in rhetoric?

No, but you need to show that you communicate well in demanding situations yourself. Many organisers ask for a video or a reference.

Is a workshop or a talk better?

It depends on the organiser. Courses and networks often like exercises, while conferences more often want a shorter talk.

Can I show client work in a talk?

Only what you are allowed to share. Ask the client or anonymise the case; organisers care more about the lesson than the logo.
