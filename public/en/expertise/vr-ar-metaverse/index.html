Speaking opportunities in VR, AR and the metaverse

Practical business applications of immersive technology.

Once the excitement around the metaverse faded, interest in immersive technology moved towards down-to-earth uses. Companies train staff for hazardous tasks in VR, field engineers see instructions in their line of sight with AR, and architects walk clients through buildings before they exist. These are the cases audiences want to hear about, ideally from someone who has deployed the technology for real and can describe what it delivered, how users felt about wearing headsets and why some projects were shelved.

Technical universities and courses in design and game development invite practitioners, and student societies love it when the audience can try the kit themselves. Industry bodies in manufacturing, construction and education hold seminars comparing applications, and meetups bring developers together. If you plan a demo, the logistics are yours to sort out, and organisers want honesty about both cost and limitations.

Questions

Should I bring equipment for a demo?

It often makes a talk more memorable, but agree on room, timing and tech with the organiser well in advance. A short video is a good fallback.

Is the metaverse still a topic that gets booked?

Less often on its own. Organisers prefer concrete uses of VR and AR to broad visions of the future.

Do I need to be a developer to speak about AI?

No. Organisers also want people who have led AI projects, bought tools, handled the risks or changed how a team works. Real experience matters more than code.
