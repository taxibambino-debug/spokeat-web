Speaking opportunities in mental resilience

Building resilience and recovering from setbacks.

Reorganisations, redundancies, failed projects and unexpected life events affect most people at some point in their working lives. That is why many want to hear how others have dealt with setbacks and found a way forward. Talks that get booked rest on a real experience the speaker has worked through and can describe with some distance, or on professional knowledge from fields such as leadership, sport or research. Organisers pass on messages suggesting that anyone can simply think their way out of a crisis.

Student societies, industry associations, leadership networks and working-life podcasts book the subject, often as a talk or an on-stage conversation. Organisers check a speaker's background and expect you to stay within your own expertise and experience. Advice on mental health and treatment belongs to licensed professionals, and a responsible talk does not promise to make the audience more resilient.

Questions

Can I speak about resilience based on a personal crisis?

Yes, if you have worked through the experience and can describe it with some distance. Make clear it is your story and not mental health advice.

What background do organisers look for?

Either relevant professional knowledge or a clear personal experience. Organisers often ask about your background before booking.

Can I speak about stress or wellbeing without being a clinician?

Only within your own role and experience, for example as a manager describing measures you introduced. Clinical advice belongs to licensed professionals.
