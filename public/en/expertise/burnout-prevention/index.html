Speaking opportunities in burnout prevention

Tools for recovery and for preventing burnout.

The question of how workplaces can prevent burnout has moved from the HR department to the leadership team. Managers and staff want to understand what an organisation can do before anyone falls ill, and they want to hear it from someone who has worked on the issue for real. Talks that get booked usually deal with workload, priorities and culture, told by a manager, HR lead or occupational health professional, or by someone sharing a personal experience thoughtfully and without generalising.

HR networks, industry associations and courses for future managers book the subject, as do podcasts about working life. Organisers check a speaker's background and expect you to stay within your own expertise and established knowledge. Diagnosis, treatment and individual advice belong to licensed professionals, and a responsible talk does not promise the audience they will stay well.

Questions

Can I speak about burnout from my own experience?

Yes, as a personal story, but make clear you are not giving medical advice. Point the audience to healthcare or occupational health services for their own needs.

What do organisers expect from a speaker on this subject?

A relevant background, a responsible tone and staying within what you know. Many organisers ask about your experience before booking.

Can I speak about stress or wellbeing without being a clinician?

Only within your own role and experience, for example as a manager describing measures you introduced. Clinical advice belongs to licensed professionals.
