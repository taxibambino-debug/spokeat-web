Speaking opportunities in data-driven decision making

How organisations put big data to use in practice.

Nearly every organisation collects more data than ever, yet few feel their decisions have improved. Reports multiply while the question of who actually changes course after reading them goes unanswered. That is why organisers are drawn to speakers who can show one decision genuinely shaped by data: the question that was asked, how the evidence was built, where the numbers misled and how leadership came to trust the analysis. Candour about poor data quality is often what sticks with an audience.

Universities and business schools invite practitioners into courses on analytics, statistics and management control, and data and economics student societies enjoy running case evenings. Meetups gather analysts who want technical depth, while industry seminars and webinars are aimed at managers. Match the level to the room and explain the reasoning behind the numbers rather than showing as many charts as possible.

Questions

Do I need deep statistical knowledge?

Not for every audience. Management seminars mostly want to hear how data fed into decisions, while meetups and analytics courses expect more on method.

Can I show real data from my organisation?

Only with permission. Anonymised or invented examples usually work just as well to show the reasoning.

Do I need to be a developer to speak about AI?

No. Organisers also want people who have led AI projects, bought tools, handled the risks or changed how a team works. Real experience matters more than code.
