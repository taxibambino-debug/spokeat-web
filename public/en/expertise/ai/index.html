Speaking opportunities for AI and digitalisation experts

AI is the topic organisers are asked about most right now, from developer conferences to boardroom breakfasts. That demand is real, but so is the noise: many talks promise transformation and show little. Organisers increasingly look for speakers who have put AI or digital tools into real work and can say what it cost, what broke and what changed.

That makes practitioners valuable. Universities and business schools want guests who connect courses to real deployments. Developer conferences and meetups run open calls through the year. Industry associations and breakfast seminars want plain-language talks for people who must decide, buy or regulate. Webinars and podcasts suit short, practical formats.

Questions

Do I need to be a developer to speak about AI?

No. Organisers also want people who have led AI projects, bought tools, handled the risks or changed how a team works. Real experience matters more than code.

Where are AI calls for speakers published?

Many conferences use Sessionize or their own call page, often months ahead. Our weekly post lists newly opened calls every Friday.

How do I avoid sounding like every other AI talk?

Bring one real case with numbers you are allowed to share, including what did not work.
