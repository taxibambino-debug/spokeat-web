Speaking opportunities in psychological safety

How to build high-performing teams through trust.

Psychological safety is a term most leaders have now heard, but it is often misread as everyone being nice and avoiding awkward conversations. Audiences want to understand what it looks like in practice: someone daring to say the project will not hold, mistakes reported early, criticism passed upwards without fear. Organisers say yes when the speaker can describe a team that changed and the specific actions that made the difference, rather than only the research behind the idea.

Leadership courses at universities and business schools, HR networks and industry associations in sectors such as healthcare and technology book it regularly. It also suits workshops and internal leadership days where people work on their own situations. Treat the subject seriously without turning it into therapy, and be ready for the familiar objection that safety comes at the expense of standards and results.

Questions

Do I need to know the research on psychological safety?

Knowing the basics helps, but you are booked for your experience. Refer to the research briefly and spend the time on what you have seen yourself.

Does the topic work for audiences who are not managers?

Yes. Team members shape the climate too, and many organisers want angles aimed at the whole group.

Do I need a leadership title to speak about leadership?

No, but you need first-hand experience to draw on. Organisers book specific stories from people who have led teams, projects or change, not general advice.
