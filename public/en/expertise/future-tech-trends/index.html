Speaking opportunities in future tech trends

Deep dives into the next big technology shifts, such as quantum computing.

Quantum computing, advanced robotics and new materials sound like science fiction, but for a growing number of organisations the question is becoming practical: when will this affect us, and what should we do now? Audiences want help telling real breakthroughs from headlines. Organisers choose speakers who work close to the technology, as researchers, engineers or investors, and can explain where it genuinely stands today, what obstacles remain and which signals are worth watching.

Universities and technical institutes invite speakers who connect research to application, and science and engineering student societies enjoy evenings full of curious questions. Conferences often run inspiration or foresight tracks, and podcasts allow room for longer reasoning. Difficult ideas should become clear without the uncertainty being smoothed away, and anything speculative needs to be labelled as such.

Questions

Do I have to be a researcher to speak about emerging technology?

No, but you need to work close to the technology or its applications. Organisers quickly notice the difference between first-hand insight and summarised news.

How do I talk about something that does not exist yet?

Be clear about what is proven, what is likely and what is guesswork. Audiences appreciate that honesty.

Do I need to be a developer to speak about AI?

No. Organisers also want people who have led AI projects, bought tools, handled the risks or changed how a team works. Real experience matters more than code.
