Speaking opportunities in net zero and the climate transition

Strategies for how companies reduce and manage their carbon emissions.

Many companies have pledged net zero, and customers, lenders and staff are now asking what is actually happening. Organisers look for speakers who can show real emission reductions: cleaner energy in their own operations, changes to what they buy and work with suppliers. Talks that get booked start from measurable change and treat carbon offsetting with caution, as something that comes after reductions and is never used to call a product climate neutral.

Demand comes from energy and environmental engineering courses, strategy programmes at business schools and climate-focused student societies. Trade associations in manufacturing, property and transport hold seminars on the transition, and webinars attract decision-makers who want to compare approaches. Organisers expect you to separate targets from results, be open about uncertainty and avoid wording that could be read as greenwashing.

Questions

Can I speak about net zero if we have not reached our target?

Yes. Most organisations are mid-way, and an honest account of where you stand and what is hard carries more weight than a success story.

How do I discuss offsetting without greenwashing?

Start with what you have actually reduced, be clear about what offsets do not achieve and avoid claims of climate neutrality you cannot back up.

What do sustainability organisers want from practitioners?

Concrete experience of delivery: data, trade-offs, costs and results, including what did not work.
