Speaking opportunities in imposter syndrome

Overcoming the feeling of being a fraud in your career.

The feeling of not quite belonging in your role, or of being about to be found out, is familiar to many professionals, especially after a promotion, a change of industry or when you are the first person with your background in the room. Imposter syndrome is not a diagnosis but a name for a common experience. Organisers book speakers who can talk honestly about how the feeling has affected their working life and what helped them keep taking up space.

Student societies, networks for women or young professionals, industry associations and career podcasts often book the subject. Organisers check a speaker's background and expect you to stay with your own experience or research you can cite. If the subject moves into mental ill health, advice and assessment belong to licensed professionals, and a serious talk does not promise the feeling will go away.

Questions

Is imposter syndrome a diagnosis?

No. It describes an experience, and talks should not present it as a diagnosis or offer treatment advice.

Can I speak about it without training in psychology?

Yes, if you draw on your own experience and say so. Refer clinical questions to licensed professionals.

Can I speak about stress or wellbeing without being a clinician?

Only within your own role and experience, for example as a manager describing measures you introduced. Clinical advice belongs to licensed professionals.
