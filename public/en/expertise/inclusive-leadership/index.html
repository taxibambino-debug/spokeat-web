Speaking opportunities in inclusive leadership (DEI)

Practical work on diversity, inclusion and equality.

Questions of diversity, equality and inclusion stir strong feelings in several directions, so many organisations want less debate and more practice. What they look for are speakers who can show how the work happens day to day: how a recruitment process was made fairer, how equal pay is followed up, or how a manager handles a situation where someone feels left out. Talks get booked when they are concrete, measured and leave the audience room to draw their own conclusions.

Leadership courses at universities and business schools invite practitioners, and HR networks and industry associations run seminars and workshops for managers. Student societies often host panels where different experiences and views can meet. Organisers want you to be factual and respectful towards differing perspectives, to separate your own experience from general claims, and to focus on ways of working a manager could use tomorrow.

Questions

How do I handle critical questions from the audience?

Listen, answer factually and stay within your own experience. It is perfectly reasonable to say a question lies outside what you can speak to.

Do I need to belong to a particular group to speak about inclusion?

No. Organisers mainly look for practical experience of the work, for example as a manager, recruiter or HR lead.

Do I need a leadership title to speak about leadership?

No, but you need first-hand experience to draw on. Organisers book specific stories from people who have led teams, projects or change, not general advice.
