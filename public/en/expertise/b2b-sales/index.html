Speaking opportunities in modern B2B sales

Social selling and digital sales processes.

B2B buyers now do much of their homework before they ever speak to a salesperson, and that has changed the job in ways people want explained. Students heading into commercial roles and experienced sellers alike want to hear how selling works when the customer already knows a lot and has little time. Talks get booked when they rest on things you have done: building a pipeline from nothing, losing a large deal at the last step, or moving a team from cold calls to relationships built over months in digital channels.

Business schools and sales programmes invite practitioners for guest lectures, while sales and marketing associations look for speakers for breakfast seminars and webinars. Sales podcasts are always after guests with candid stories. Organisers expect you to share real examples without exposing clients, and to handle questions from an audience that sells for a living.

Questions

Can I talk about sales without revealing clients?

Yes. Anonymise clients and figures and focus on what you did and learned. Organisers care more about the process than the names.

Is it fine to talk about sales tools?

Yes, as long as the tool is an example rather than the message. A talk that sounds like a product demo is usually turned down.

Can I show client work in a talk?

Only what you are allowed to share. Ask the client or anonymise the case; organisers care more about the lesson than the logo.
