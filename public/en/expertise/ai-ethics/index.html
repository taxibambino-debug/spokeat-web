Speaking opportunities in AI ethics and regulation

Handling bias, ethics and compliance in machine learning.

New EU rules on AI and growing concern about unfair algorithms have moved ethics out of the philosophy seminar and onto the leadership agenda. Organisations need to know what is required of them, but even more they want to know what to do when a model produces skewed results or nobody can explain an automated decision. The speakers who get invited have audited a system, drafted internal guidelines or sat through the hard conversations between lawyers, engineers and the business.

Universities bring practitioners into courses on data ethics, law and information systems, and engineering and law student societies run panels where these perspectives can rub against each other. Industry associations and seminars want sessions that help members get ready. Organisers count on you to separate what the rules actually say from your own reading of them, and not to give legal advice from the stage.

Questions

Do I need to be a lawyer to speak on AI regulation?

No, but be clear about your role. Many organisers want to hear how the rules play out inside a business, and experience from product, data or compliance carries just as much weight.

How do I keep an ethics talk from becoming too abstract?

Build it around a real case you worked on and show the trade-offs you made. Audiences remember concrete dilemmas far better than principles.

Do I need to be a developer to speak about AI?

No. Organisers also want people who have led AI projects, bought tools, handled the risks or changed how a team works. Real experience matters more than code.
