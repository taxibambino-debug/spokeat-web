Speaking opportunities in sustainable supply chains

Ethical, green and environmentally friendly logistics.

Customers, investors and new expectations on supplier due diligence mean companies must know far more about their suppliers than before, often several tiers back. At the same time, freight costs and disruption to global trade squeeze margins. Organisers book speakers who can show how risk mapping works in practice, how to set requirements without losing key suppliers, and how to cut emissions from transport and warehousing.

Logistics and procurement programmes, operations management courses at business schools and engineering student societies are keen to invite practitioners. Purchasing and logistics networks hold seminars and industry days, and podcasts on trade and transport look for guests with concrete examples. Organisers expect you to cover both ethical and environmental trade-offs and to be open about what you still cannot see or control.

Questions

Can I name suppliers in a talk?

Be careful and check with your employer first. Anonymised examples usually work just as well and protect the relationship.

Does the subject work as a webinar?

Yes, especially for buyers who want practical tools. A short walk-through of one method and one real example tends to work best.

What do sustainability organisers want from practitioners?

Concrete experience of delivery: data, trade-offs, costs and results, including what did not work.
