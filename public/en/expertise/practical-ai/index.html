Speaking opportunities in practical AI integration

How generative AI makes everyday work more efficient for businesses.

Most organisations have tried generative AI by now, yet far fewer have made it part of ordinary work. Audiences want to hear from someone who has done that properly: which tasks genuinely got quicker, where the tools gave wrong answers and how staff reacted when routines changed. A talk gets booked when it walks through a real workflow before and after, with honest detail on time, cost and the ideas that had to be dropped.

Industry associations and breakfast seminars book this most often, because their members have to decide what to buy and roll out. Business schools and professional courses invite practitioners into digitalisation modules, and webinars suit the subject well since you can show the tools on screen. You will need to speak plainly, avoid pitching any particular vendor and handle questions on data protection and accountability.

Questions

Do I need a technical background to speak on practical AI?

No. Organisers often want the person who led adoption in a team or business, as long as you can explain clearly what the tools did and did not do.

Should I demo tools live?

It often goes down well, especially in webinars. Keep recorded screenshots ready in case the connection or the tool lets you down.

Do I need to be a developer to speak about AI?

No. Organisers also want people who have led AI projects, bought tools, handled the risks or changed how a team works. Real experience matters more than code.
