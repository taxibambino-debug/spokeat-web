Speaking opportunities in Web3 and blockchain

The decentralised economy and the future of digital ownership.

After years of inflated expectations and painful setbacks, interest in blockchain has become more sober. Audiences no longer want visions of decentralising everything; they want to understand what the technology is genuinely used for, such as tokenised assets, payments, traceability and digital ownership people can rely on. Speakers get booked when they can describe an application that is running, what it took to make it work, and where the technology turned out to be the wrong answer.

Universities and business schools invite practitioners into finance, innovation and information systems courses, and economics and engineering student societies like evening sessions with plenty of questions. Meetups and developer conferences want technical depth, while industry seminars and podcasts look for someone who can explain it to decision makers. Balance matters, as does openness about your own interests, and investment advice has no place in the talk.

Questions

Should I disclose my own holdings or affiliations?

Being open about them is wise. Organisers and audiences trust a speaker more when they are clear about where they stand.

How do I explain the technology to a non-specialist audience?

Start with the problem it solves and hold back on the jargon. An everyday comparison usually works better than a technical walkthrough.

Do I need to be a developer to speak about AI?

No. Organisers also want people who have led AI projects, bought tools, handled the risks or changed how a team works. Real experience matters more than code.
