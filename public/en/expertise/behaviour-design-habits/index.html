Speaking opportunities in behaviour design and habits

The science behind how we make lasting lifestyle changes.

Organisations and individuals alike want to understand why change so often fizzles out, whether it concerns new ways of working, digital tools or everyday habits. Interest in behavioural science has grown, but audiences have also become more sceptical of simplified recipes. Talks that get booked come from speakers with relevant training, or from practitioners who have worked on behaviour change in fields such as product development, change management or public health and can describe what worked and what did not.

University courses, HR and change management networks, product meetups and podcasts book the subject. Organisers check a speaker's background and expect you to stay within your own expertise and to separate what research shows from interpretation. Advice on health, diet or treatment for individuals belongs to licensed professionals, and a serious talk promises no particular outcomes.

Questions

Do I need an academic background to speak on behaviour design?

It helps, but practitioners can be booked too if they say which research they rely on and stay within their field. Organisers often check backgrounds.

Can I give advice on health habits?

Keep to principles and examples from your own field. Individual health advice belongs to licensed professionals.

Can I speak about stress or wellbeing without being a clinician?

Only within your own role and experience, for example as a manager describing measures you introduced. Clinical advice belongs to licensed professionals.
