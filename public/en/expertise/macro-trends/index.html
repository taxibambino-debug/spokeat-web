Speaking opportunities in macroeconomic trends

How the global economy and inflation affect business.

Inflation, interest rates, currencies and trade policy have quickly become everyday questions for leadership teams. Audiences want to know what the big shifts mean for their own sector: pricing, wage setting, investment decisions and demand. A talk gets booked when the speaker links the macro picture to concrete decisions, explains without jargon and stays humble about how hard the next turn is to predict.

Industry associations, chambers of commerce and regional business networks often book the subject for breakfast briefings and annual meetings. Economics courses and student finance societies want to see how theory is used in real analysis, and economics podcasts look for guests who can explain current events. Organisers expect a balanced view built on several scenarios rather than a single forecast, and clarity about whom you represent.

Questions

Do I need to be an economist to speak on macro trends?

Not always. A purchasing or finance director who shows how the trends hit the business is often as much in demand as an analyst.

How current does the talk need to be?

Very. Update figures and examples shortly before each booking, because the audience has often read the same news as you.

Do big fintech conferences charge speakers?

Some do, for example through a discounted speaker pass. Our posts show what the speaker pays or gets, so you can decide before you apply.
