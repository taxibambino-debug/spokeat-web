Speaking opportunities in green technology and innovation

The role of technology in solving the climate crisis.

The climate transition has moved from pledges to investment decisions, and many people want to know which technologies are actually making a difference. Audiences tend to be wary of green marketing language and would rather listen to someone who has built, funded or deployed a solution: a new energy system in a factory, an electrified transport chain or a start-up that got from prototype to paying customer. What gets booked are stories where both the climate benefit and the business logic can be followed, including what proved harder than expected.

Technical universities and environmental and energy programmes welcome practitioners, and sustainability-minded student societies organise seminars and panels. Industry associations and conferences in energy, construction and manufacturing want concrete examples for their members, and podcasts make room for longer conversations. Be careful with claims about climate impact, explain how it is measured and distinguish targets from results achieved.

Questions

How do I avoid being seen as greenwashing?

Be concrete about what the solution does and how its impact is measured, and be open about uncertainties. Organisers trust honest limits more than big promises.

Does the topic work for non-technical audiences?

Yes, if you start from the problem and the business case rather than the engineering. Many seminars are aimed at decision makers and investors.

What do sustainability organisers want from practitioners?

Concrete experience of delivery: data, trade-offs, costs and results, including what did not work.
