Speaking opportunities in cyber security for employees

How companies protect themselves against IT threats and social engineering.

Most breaches do not start with clever technology but with an email, a phone call or a busy employee who clicks too quickly. That is why organisations want everyone to understand the threats, not just the IT team. The talks that get booked are built on real incidents: what a fraud attempt looked like, why it nearly worked and which habits stopped it. They land best when people recognise their own working day and leave with a handful of things to do differently.

Industry associations, breakfast seminars and webinars book this often, frequently aimed at managers and owners of smaller firms. Vocational programmes and university courses in information security invite practitioners who can show the attacker's point of view. Organisers want accessible language without scare tactics, and they trust you never to reveal sensitive details about a client or employer.

Questions

Can I talk about incidents I was involved in?

Yes, but anonymise them thoroughly and check what you are allowed to share. Real examples are valued, but not at the expense of a former employer's security.

What level should I pitch at for a general audience?

Assume the audience is not technical. Focus on behaviour and warning signs rather than tools and protocols.

Can I talk about real incidents?

Only with permission and without details that create new risk. Many speakers describe a composite or a published case.
