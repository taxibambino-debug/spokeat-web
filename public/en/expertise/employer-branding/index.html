Speaking opportunities in employer branding

How companies win the competition for the best people.

Many organisations find that salary and benefits are not enough when candidates compare employers. What tips the balance is often what current staff say, how the recruitment process feels and whether the picture in the job advert matches reality. Audiences therefore want to hear from someone who has built an employer brand from scratch or turned round a poor reputation: what they changed internally first, which channels worked and how they checked they were attracting the right people.

HR networks, industry associations and recruitment seminars book this regularly, and it suits webinars and podcasts too. Student societies often hold evenings where employers talk about their work, and there the audience is also the target group, so honesty counts for even more. Share methods and lessons others can use, and do not let the talk turn into a job advert for your own employer.

Questions

Can I mention my current employer?

Yes, it is often part of the story. Just make sure the talk is about lessons rather than promotion, or you are unlikely to be invited back.

Which organisers suit me if I mainly recruit graduates?

Student societies and universities are natural choices, as are HR networks focused on early careers.

Can I speak about HR if I am a manager, not an HR professional?

Yes. Many organisers want the manager's view of hiring, onboarding or hybrid work alongside HR's.
