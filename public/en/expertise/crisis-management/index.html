Speaking opportunities in strategic crisis management

Decision making and communication under extreme pressure.

Cyber attacks, supply chain failures and sudden media storms have turned crisis readiness into a leadership issue for more and more organisations. Audiences want to hear from someone who has actually sat in the crisis team: what the first hours looked like, how decisions were made with incomplete information and what was said to staff, customers and the press. What makes a talk bookable is the reflection afterwards, meaning what worked, what failed and what the organisation changed.

Business schools and programmes in communication, security and leadership invite practitioners, and industry associations hold seminars on preparedness. Webinars and podcasts give room for the longer story. Share experience and lessons rather than ready-made solutions, make clear that every crisis needs its own professional and legal advice, and protect both confidentiality and the people who were affected.

Questions

Can I talk about a crisis that drew a lot of media attention?

Often yes, but focus on your own perspective and lessons, and steer clear of ongoing disputes or confidential details. Check with your organisation beforehand.

Should I give the audience a ready-made crisis plan?

No. Share what worked for you and why, and remind people that each organisation needs to build its own preparedness with the right expert support.

Do I need a leadership title to speak about leadership?

No, but you need first-hand experience to draw on. Organisers book specific stories from people who have led teams, projects or change, not general advice.
