Speaking opportunities in company culture

Building and sustaining strong values in an organisation.

Most organisations have values on the wall, but audiences know culture is decided by what happens when nobody is watching: who gets promoted, which behaviour is tolerated and what happens when a value costs money. That is why organisers book speakers who can describe a moment when the culture was tested. It might be a fast-growing company close to losing its identity, or an established business that had to break with old habits.

Business schools and organisation and leadership courses like inviting founders and managers with real experience, and student societies want to understand what kind of culture to look for in an employer. Start-up networks, founder meetups and HR associations host seminars and on-stage conversations. What goes down well is honesty about the gap between ideals and reality, with examples that are clear without singling out individual colleagues.

Questions

Do I need to be a chief executive to speak about company culture?

No. Managers, HR leads and employees who have driven culture work are booked too, as long as they can show concretely what changed.

How do I make a soft topic concrete?

Talk about specific decisions and events rather than describing the values. Actions are what audiences remember.

Do I need a leadership title to speak about leadership?

No, but you need first-hand experience to draw on. Organisers book specific stories from people who have led teams, projects or change, not general advice.
