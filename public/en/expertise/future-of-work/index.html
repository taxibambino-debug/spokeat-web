Speaking opportunities in the future of work

How automation and the gig economy are changing the labour market.

Automation, platform work and new kinds of contract raise questions that touch everyone: which roles disappear, which grow, and what security means when more people work on assignment. Audiences have had enough of predictions and want to hear from someone who has been in the middle of the shift. That might be a manager who reshaped a department, an HR lead who built a reskilling programme or a freelancer who can describe the reality without gloss. Lived experience is what organisers are after.

Universities and colleges invite guest lecturers into courses on working life, organisation and HR, and student societies want to know what awaits them after graduation. Trade unions and industry bodies run seminars and panels where different interests meet, and podcasts suit the topic well. Keep your own experience apart from general claims, and respect the fact that the subject makes many listeners anxious.

Questions

Do I need a research background to speak about the future of work?

No. Organisers often look for practitioners who have carried out a change and can say what happened to the people, not just the organisation chart.

How do I avoid sounding like a fortune teller?

Stick to what you have seen and done. Say what you believe and why, rather than what will definitely happen.

Can I speak about HR if I am a manager, not an HR professional?

Yes. Many organisers want the manager's view of hiring, onboarding or hybrid work alongside HR's.
