Speaking opportunities for entrepreneurship and innovation experts

Founders, investors and innovation leaders are among the most invited guests in student and startup settings. Audiences want honest accounts of building something: the first customers, the funding rounds, the product that changed direction and the mistakes that nearly ended it.

Entrepreneurship courses and programmes at universities rely on practitioners. Incubators, science parks and startup hubs run breakfast talks and pitch events. Student entrepreneurship societies invite founders regularly. Startup conferences open calls for talks and panels, and podcasts about entrepreneurship look for guests with a clear story.

Questions

Do I need a successful exit to speak?

No. Honest stories about early stages, failures and pivots are often more useful to audiences than success stories.

Where do startup events look for speakers?

Incubators and hubs mostly invite directly; conferences publish calls. Short, specific pitches work best.

Can investors speak at student events?

Yes, if the talk teaches rather than recruits deals. Organisers are wary of sales pitches.
