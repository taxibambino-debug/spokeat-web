Speaking opportunities in the circular economy

Shifting from linear production to reuse.

Plenty of companies have set targets for reuse, repair and take-back, yet few can show the business case. Organisers therefore look for speakers who have moved a real operation away from make, use and dispose: which products could come back, how returns logistics was handled and what happened to margins. Talks get booked when they rest on first-hand numbers and are candid about the parts that failed.

The subject suits courses in product design and industrial management, strategy modules at business schools and student societies running case evenings. Trade associations in retail, textiles, construction and electronics hold seminars where members want models they can take home and apply. Organisers expect you to explain the business model plainly, keep recycling and reuse distinct, and handle questions about cost.

Questions

Do I need a profitable circular model to be booked?

No. An honest account of an attempt that did not pay off is often just as welcome, as long as you show what you learned.

Which audience is best for a first booking?

Student societies and product design courses are open to practitioners and let you test your case in front of a smaller group.

What do sustainability organisers want from practitioners?

Concrete experience of delivery: data, trade-offs, costs and results, including what did not work.
