Speaking opportunities in digital transformation

Strategies for reshaping traditional business models.

Plenty of established companies have digitised parts of their operations only to find that putting new systems on top of old habits changes very little. The hard part is changing how the business earns money, makes decisions and works across departments. Organisers like to book someone who has led that kind of shift in a traditional sector such as manufacturing, retail or the public sector, and who can talk honestly about resistance, priorities and what took far longer than planned.

Business schools and industrial engineering programmes want practitioners who link strategy theory to real choices. Industry associations and executive networks hold seminars for members facing similar decisions, and conferences often have tracks devoted to the topic. You are expected to get past the buzzwords, show what actually changed in the business and be candid about what did not go to plan.

Questions

Is leading a large IT project enough to speak on this?

It can be, if you can show how the project changed the business and the way people worked. Organisers want stories of change, not of system upgrades.

Can I name my employer?

Often yes, but check with them first and leave out sensitive figures. Describing the sector and situation without names also works well.

Do I need to be a developer to speak about AI?

No. Organisers also want people who have led AI projects, bought tools, handled the risks or changed how a team works. Real experience matters more than code.
