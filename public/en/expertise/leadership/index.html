Speaking opportunities for leadership experts

Leadership is one of the broadest speaking topics there is, which makes it both easy and hard. Easy, because almost every audience cares about it: students about to become managers, associations whose members lead teams, and companies planning a kick-off. Hard, because organisers hear generic leadership talks all the time and quickly pass on anything that sounds like a book summary.

The talks that get booked are specific. They come from a real situation you have led: a turnaround, a merger, building a team from scratch, leading through a crisis or changing a culture. Business schools and management courses invite practitioners to show what theory looks like on a Monday morning. Associations and networks want peers who speak frankly. Panels at seminars and Almedalen need people who can disagree in a useful way.

Questions

Do I need a leadership title to speak about leadership?

No, but you need first-hand experience to draw on. Organisers book specific stories from people who have led teams, projects or change, not general advice.

Which formats suit leadership talks?

Guest lectures in management courses, panel debates, fireside chats and short talks at network meetings. Panels are often the easiest first slot.

How do I stand out with such a common topic?

Narrow it. Pick one situation you have led and one lesson the audience can use. A clear, narrow angle is what makes an organiser say yes.
