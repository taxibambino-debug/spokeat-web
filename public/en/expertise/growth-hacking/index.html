Speaking opportunities in growth hacking for start-ups

Data-driven, fast and cost-effective business scaling.

Start-ups on a tight budget need customers quickly, so the subject draws founders, students and marketers who want to work in a more data-led way. The term has been worn thin by inflated promises, which is why organisers look for speakers who can show how the experiments really went. What gets booked is detail: a test that changed how you won users, a channel that looked promising but never paid off, or how you built a culture where anyone could try an idea.

Incubators, entrepreneurship courses and societies for would-be founders are natural bookers, and start-up meetups often schedule short talks followed by questions. Growth and product podcasts look for guests who will speak openly about results. Organisers expect you to be specific about method and tools, to show the attempts that failed and not to promise shortcuts.

Questions

Does my start-up need to have succeeded for me to speak?

No. Lessons from a company that did not survive can be very valuable, as long as you can explain what you learned.

How technical should the talk be?

Match the audience. Students and generalists want principles, while specialist meetups often want to see method and tools.

Do I need a successful exit to speak?

No. Honest stories about early stages, failures and pivots are often more useful to audiences than success stories.
