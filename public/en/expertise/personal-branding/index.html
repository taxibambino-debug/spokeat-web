Speaking opportunities in personal branding

Building authority on LinkedIn and becoming a thought leader.

More professionals now see that their reputation in an industry shapes the jobs, clients and invitations that come their way. Many are also unsure how to be visible without feeling fake. That is why organisers want someone who has built a name in their field and can be frank about what it cost in time and what it returned. A talk that gets booked is not about being famous. It is about how you went from unknown outside your own office to the person people call.

Student societies and careers fairs book this often, because students want to know how to stand out before their first job. Professional networks and industry associations like short talks and workshops, and career podcasts look for guests. Organisers expect concrete examples from your own path and honesty about what did not work.

Questions

Do I need a large following to speak about personal branding?

No. What matters is showing how visibility has affected your career or business. A small but relevant audience is often a strong example.

Should the talk focus on one platform?

Use examples from it if you like, but build the talk around principles that still hold when platforms change.

Can I show client work in a talk?

Only what you are allowed to share. Ask the client or anonymise the case; organisers care more about the lesson than the logo.
