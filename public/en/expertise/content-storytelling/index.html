Speaking opportunities in content marketing and storytelling

Content strategies that engage and convert.

Most companies publish more content than ever, yet few can show that it brings in business. That is why there is real interest in speakers who can explain why some articles, videos and podcasts stick while the rest disappear into the feed. What persuades an organiser is a story about your own work: a content strategy that turned a trend around, a series that built trust in a sceptical sector, or a project that flopped and taught you a lot.

Marketing courses at universities and vocational colleges invite practitioners, and communications networks run breakfast seminars and meetups where examples count most. Webinars suit the subject because you can show material on screen. Organisers like before-and-after examples and expect you to separate what brought reach from what actually brought customers.

Questions

Can I show client work in my talk?

Only with permission. Otherwise describe the approach and show your own or anonymised examples.

Is storytelling on its own a strong enough topic?

It is broad. Tie it to an audience or a problem, such as storytelling for technical products, and it becomes easier to book.

Can I show client work in a talk?

Only what you are allowed to share. Ask the client or anonymise the case; organisers care more about the lesson than the logo.
