Speaking opportunities in social entrepreneurship

Running profitable companies with a higher social purpose.

A growing number of people, students and early-career professionals in particular, want to build companies that tackle a social problem and still pay their way. They want to hear from founders who have made that journey: how to price when the customer and the beneficiary are different people, how to measure impact without drowning in reporting, and how to handle the pull between purpose and profit. Honesty about hard decisions is what makes a talk stick.

Entrepreneurship societies and courses in innovation and business development are the most common hosts. Meetups for impact founders and podcasts on starting a business also look for guests. Organisers want a concrete account of the business model and funding rather than a general discussion of social issues, and they value speakers who stay on to answer questions from aspiring founders.

Questions

Does my company need to be profitable for me to be booked?

Not necessarily. Organisers want to see a credible route to sound finances and hear how you have thought it through.

How do I keep the talk from becoming ideological?

Stay with the problem you solve, the business model and the results. Let the audience draw its own conclusions about wider social questions.

What do sustainability organisers want from practitioners?

Concrete experience of delivery: data, trade-offs, costs and results, including what did not work.
