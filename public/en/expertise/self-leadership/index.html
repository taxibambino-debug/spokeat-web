Speaking opportunities in self-leadership

Taking ownership of your own time, energy and development.

As more people work independently, remotely or in flat organisations, the demands on each person to steer their own time and development grow. The subject therefore engages students about to start work as much as experienced specialists. Talks that get booked rest on real choices: how you took charge of your career when nobody else did, how you turned down the wrong assignments, or how you built routines that held up through a demanding period.

Student societies, university careers services and professional networks often book the subject, and it works as a workshop as well as a talk. Career and working-life podcasts look for guests with their own stories. Organisers expect concrete examples, honesty about what did not work and no suggestion that your own path is the only right one.

Questions

Is self-leadership a topic for managers or for everyone?

For everyone. It works especially well for students, specialists and consultants who work independently.

Does the subject suit a workshop format?

Yes. Many organisers like a format where participants reflect and discuss rather than only listen.

Do I need a leadership title to speak about leadership?

No, but you need first-hand experience to draw on. Organisers book specific stories from people who have led teams, projects or change, not general advice.
