Speaking opportunities in applied creativity

Methods for unlocking innovative thinking on demand.

Organisations are expected to renew themselves faster, yet few have a clear method for producing ideas when they are needed. That creates interest in speakers who can show that creativity can be worked on systematically instead of waiting for inspiration. Talks that get booked draw on real projects: an ideas workshop that led to a new service, a team that learned to test ideas early, or an innovation project that stalled and why.

Design programmes, entrepreneurship courses, innovation networks and meetups book the subject, often as an interactive workshop. Student societies running hackathons and case competitions also look for speakers. Organisers expect you to be able to lead a group through an exercise, to ground the methods in your own work and to be honest that creative processes can fail too.

Questions

Do I need to work in a creative industry?

No. Engineers, managers and specialists who have led innovation work in traditional organisations are often especially interesting to organisers.

How interactive should the session be?

It depends on the format. A workshop calls for exercises, while a talk can include one short exercise to show the method.

Do I need a successful exit to speak?

No. Honest stories about early stages, failures and pivots are often more useful to audiences than success stories.
