Speaking opportunities in neuromarketing

Consumer psychology and the science behind buying decisions.

There is wide interest in how people really make buying decisions, among marketers and among students who want to understand more than click rates. The subject also invites hard questions about where understanding customers ends and manipulating them begins. Talks that get booked pair research-based knowledge with your own application: how you tested a message, what changed when you made an offer easier to understand, or why an effect seen in a lab did not hold up in the market.

University courses in marketing and consumer behaviour invite practitioners and researchers, and marketing networks run seminars where ethics often comes up. Webinars and podcasts suit deeper discussion. Organisers expect you to separate what the research shows from your interpretation, to stay within your own field and to treat ethics seriously rather than offering tricks for fooling customers.

Questions

Do I need a research background to speak on neuromarketing?

Not necessarily, but you need to say which research you rely on and be clear about what is your own experience. Organisers often check a speaker's background.

How should I handle ethical questions from the audience?

Raise them yourself in the talk. Showing where you draw the line makes you more credible than avoiding the question.

Can I show client work in a talk?

Only what you are allowed to share. Ask the client or anonymise the case; organisers care more about the lesson than the logo.
