Speaking opportunities in e-commerce and social commerce

Selling directly through social media and digital platforms.

The line between social media and shop is blurring, and many retailers are trying to work out how to sell where their customers already scroll. That makes the subject popular with students and with business owners planning their next move. Talks that get booked are based on running a store yourself: how you launched an online shop, how a social campaign really affected sales, or what happened to margins when returns rose and advertising got more expensive.

Entrepreneurship programmes, vocational e-commerce courses and marketing student societies like to book practitioners. Retail associations and local business networks hold seminars, and webinars work well because the audience is often spread out. Organisers expect you to cover both wins and costly mistakes and to speak about logistics and margins as readily as marketing.

Questions

Do I need to run a large online shop to get booked?

No. A small shop with honest lessons often interests audiences more than a big player showing only successes.

Can I mention my own products?

Yes, as examples, but the talk must not become a sales pitch. Organisers want the audience to leave with something useful.

Can I show client work in a talk?

Only what you are allowed to share. Ask the client or anonymise the case; organisers care more about the lesson than the logo.
