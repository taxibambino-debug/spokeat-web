Speaking opportunities in generations at work

How to lead and attract Gen Z and millennials.

With four generations working side by side, expectations around feedback, flexibility and what a job should offer sometimes clash. Many managers want to understand younger colleagues without falling back on stereotypes, and many young professionals want to be understood without being reduced to a label. The talks that get booked avoid sweeping claims and draw on direct experience: how a mixed team was made to work, why young talent walked out and what persuaded others to stay.

Student societies and their careers fairs are natural venues, as the audience belongs to the younger generations and is happy to challenge the speaker. HR networks and industry associations book the subject for seminars on recruitment and leadership, and podcasts suit conversations across age groups. Nuance is essential, no generation should be made a joke of, and audiences appreciate having their say.

Questions

How do I avoid generalising about generations?

Work from concrete situations you have been part of and make clear that individuals differ. Organisers value nuance over labels.

Can I speak on this if I am young myself?

Yes, an insider view is often in demand. Combine your own experience with what you have seen among managers and colleagues.

Can I speak about HR if I am a manager, not an HR professional?

Yes. Many organisers want the manager's view of hiring, onboarding or hybrid work alongside HR's.
