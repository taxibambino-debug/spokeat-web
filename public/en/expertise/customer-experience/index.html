Speaking opportunities in customer experience (CX)

Designing world-class customer journeys and service.

As products and prices become harder to tell apart, the experience often decides which company a customer chooses and whether they return. Management teams, students and frontline staff want to know how to actually improve a customer journey, not just map it. Talks that get booked start from real change work: how you found the step where customers gave up, how the service team was given more authority, or what happened when you stopped measuring the wrong things.

Business schools and service design programmes welcome guest speakers from the field, and associations in retail, hospitality and services run seminars and conference sessions. Podcasts on leadership and customer focus also look for guests. Organisers expect you to cover both the customer's and the employees' side and to be clear about which part was your contribution.

Questions

Do I need a CX job title to speak on this?

No. Many good speakers come from customer service, retail, product or management. What matters is that you led a concrete improvement.

How do I show results without sharing internal figures?

Describe the direction and rough scale of the change in general terms and focus on what you did differently.

Can I show client work in a talk?

Only what you are allowed to share. Ask the client or anonymise the case; organisers care more about the lesson than the logo.
