Speaking opportunities in personal finance and investing

Saving, shares and building long-term financial security.

Interest in saving, funds and shares is high, and many people want to understand the basics without being sold to. Organisers look for speakers who can explain risk, fees, time horizons and tax rules clearly, ideally with a background in advice, banking or consumer education. The talk should be education, not individual investment advice, and it gets booked when the speaker is clear that nobody can promise returns.

Student societies, evening seminars, meetups and webinars are common formats, as are personal finance podcasts. Organisers expect you not to recommend specific products to the audience, because advice on particular financial products to the public can be regulated. You should also disclose any links to a bank, fund manager or other firm that could colour what you say.

Questions

Can I tip specific shares or funds?

Avoid it. Stick to principles and general examples, because advice on specific products to the public can require authorisation.

Do I have to say where I work?

Yes, if your employer or other roles could influence the content. Being open about conflicts of interest builds trust with organisers and audiences alike.

Do big fintech conferences charge speakers?

Some do, for example through a discounted speaker pass. Our posts show what the speaker pays or gets, so you can decide before you apply.
