Speaking opportunities in conflict management

Turning internal tension into productive collaboration.

Workplace conflict is rarely dramatic, but it drains energy from every meeting where two people avoid each other or the same argument comes round again. Many managers were never taught how to handle it and keep putting it off. Audiences therefore want to hear from someone who has been in the middle of a conflict and got through it: what sparked it, which conversation turned things round and what they would now do sooner.

Leadership courses and programmes in HR and the working environment invite practitioners, and HR networks and industry associations run seminars and workshops where people practise on their own situations. Student societies value the subject ahead of group projects and first jobs. Protect the identities of those involved, distinguish everyday friction from more serious problems that need other support, and give the audience something to try straight away.

Questions

Can I use examples from my own workplace?

Yes, if they are anonymised so nobody can recognise those involved. Change details that do not affect the lesson.

Does the topic suit interactive formats?

Very well. Workshops and short exercises where the audience tries a difficult conversation are often valued more than a straight lecture.

Do I need a leadership title to speak about leadership?

No, but you need first-hand experience to draw on. Organisers book specific stories from people who have led teams, projects or change, not general advice.
