Speaking opportunities in brain science and stress

How we protect the brain in a high-performance working life.

Many workplaces have noticed that a fast pace and constant connectivity wear people down, and there is strong interest in what research says about the brain and stress. Audiences are also tired of simplified messages and quick fixes. Talks that get booked come from people with relevant training or a research background, or from practitioners who clearly separate what studies show from what they have seen in their own work, for example as a manager or in workplace health and safety.

Universities, HR networks, industry associations and in-house seminars book the subject, often as a talk with questions or as a webinar. Organisers check a speaker's background and expect you to stay within your own expertise and the evidence you can cite. Clinical advice and judgements about an individual's health belong to licensed professionals, and a serious talk promises no outcomes.

Questions

Do I have to be a researcher or doctor to speak on the brain and stress?

Not always, but you must stay within your own expertise and say which research you rely on. Medical and clinical advice belongs to licensed professionals.

Can I share my own experience of stress?

Yes, as a personal story. Make clear it is your experience and not advice that suits everyone.

Can I speak about stress or wellbeing without being a clinician?

Only within your own role and experience, for example as a manager describing measures you introduced. Clinical advice belongs to licensed professionals.
