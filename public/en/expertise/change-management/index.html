Speaking opportunities in change management

Steering organisations through crises and restructuring.

Reorganisations, cutbacks and mergers are something most professionals go through several times, and often they feel it was handled badly. That creates strong demand for speakers who carried the responsibility themselves and can describe it from the inside: how the news was delivered, how they looked after those who stayed and which decisions they would now make differently. Honesty about the hard parts is what gets a change talk booked.

Business schools and organisation theory courses invite practitioners who can put a face to the models. Executive networks, HR associations and industry seminars look for speakers ahead of their own changes, and panels work well when different roles such as leadership, HR and employee representatives share a stage. Speak respectfully about the people affected and never reveal anything that could identify individuals.

Questions

How open can I be about a restructuring I led?

Be open about your own actions and lessons, but protect individuals and confidential information. Check with your former employer if you are unsure.

Which audiences suit this topic best?

Managers and HR professionals facing their own changes are the most common audience. Students on leadership courses also value real stories.

Do I need a leadership title to speak about leadership?

No, but you need first-hand experience to draw on. Organisers book specific stories from people who have led teams, projects or change, not general advice.
