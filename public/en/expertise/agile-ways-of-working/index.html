Speaking opportunities in agile ways of working

Flexibility, speed and modern project management.

Agile methods have spread far beyond software, into marketing, HR and public administration. At the same time many organisations adopted the rituals without gaining the speed, and audiences want to know why. Speakers are invited when they can describe what happened after the rollout: how leadership was persuaded to stop micromanaging, where the method clashed with the budget cycle and what the teams themselves made of it. An honest account of both wins and trade-offs is what draws people in.

Developer and project management meetups and conferences often run open calls for speakers, and vocational and university project management courses welcome practitioners. Industry associations and webinars target managers considering agile work outside IT. Assume some prior knowledge with a specialist crowd but explain the terms for a broader one, and do not sell any single framework as the only answer.

Questions

Do I need a certification to speak about agile?

No, organisers care more about what you have delivered than about certificates. Experience across several teams or organisations carries weight.

How do I keep the topic fresh for an experienced audience?

Focus on a concrete problem you solved or failed to solve. Seasoned listeners want something new, not a recap of the basics.

Do I need a leadership title to speak about leadership?

No, but you need first-hand experience to draw on. Organisers book specific stories from people who have led teams, projects or change, not general advice.
