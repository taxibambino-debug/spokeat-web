Speaking opportunities in venture capital and investor pitching

How startups raise capital effectively.

Raising capital has become harder and more selective, and founders want to know what investors actually look for. Audiences want to hear from someone who has sat on both sides of the table, or who has raised several rounds and can talk about the yeses and the noes. A talk gets booked when it covers concrete steps: building an investor list, what happens in due diligence and how terms are negotiated.

Entrepreneurship societies, courses in entrepreneurship and corporate finance, and business school startup programmes are typical hosts. Startup meetups often run pitch nights where an experienced speaker opens the evening or sits on the judging panel, and conferences for growth companies have their own stages for funding. Organisers expect you to share real experience, respect confidentiality around companies and investors, and be open about your own roles.

Questions

Do I need an exit behind me to speak about fundraising?

No. Founders who have just closed a round are often more relevant to the audience because the experience is fresh.

Can I use real figures from our rounds?

Only if the board and investors have agreed. Approximate ranges and anonymised examples are usually enough.

Do I need a successful exit to speak?

No. Honest stories about early stages, failures and pivots are often more useful to audiences than success stories.
