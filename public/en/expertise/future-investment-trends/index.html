Speaking opportunities in future investment trends

Analysis of growth sectors such as the space economy and renewable energy.

The space economy, renewable energy, energy storage and new medical technology draw both capital and curiosity. Audiences want to understand what drives these sectors, how long the lead times are and where the risks sit. Speakers who get booked have worked close to the industries, as analysts, investors or operators, and can tell a lasting shift from a short-lived wave of expectations. The talk should be education rather than individual investment advice, and it promises no returns.

Business schools, student finance societies, industry conferences and investor seminars are the usual hosts, and podcasts on economics and technology look for guests who know a single sector in depth. Organisers expect you not to single out specific securities for the audience, since advice on products to the public can be regulated, and to disclose holdings or roles that could influence your view.

Questions

Can I mention companies I have invested in myself?

Yes, if you say so openly and use them as examples rather than recommendations. Never urge the audience to buy or sell.

How do I avoid sounding like a sales pitch?

Show both opportunities and risks, be open about uncertainty and stay at sector level rather than discussing individual securities.

Do big fintech conferences charge speakers?

Some do, for example through a discounted speaker pass. Our posts show what the speaker pays or gets, so you can decide before you apply.
