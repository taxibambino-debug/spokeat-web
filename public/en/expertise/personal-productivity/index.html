Speaking opportunities in personal productivity

Strategies for managing digital distractions and staying focused.

Notifications, chats and meetings fill the working day, and many people feel constantly busy yet rarely get to what matters most. That is why students and professionals alike want to hear how others protect their time and attention. What gets a talk booked is not another list of apps but an honest account of what you have tried: how your team cut back on meetings, how you organise a week across several roles, or which methods you dropped.

Student societies, careers fairs, professional networks and company seminars book the subject, and short webinars suit an audience that is itself short of time. Working-life podcasts look for guests with practical habits. Organisers expect you to show what it looks like in practice, to admit that what works for you will not suit everyone and to steer clear of promises about getting twice as much done.

Questions

Do I need to be an expert in productivity methods?

No. Organisers often book practitioners who show how they work under pressure, as long as the examples are concrete.

How do I stop the talk being too generic?

Start from a specific role or situation, such as managers with back-to-back meetings or consultants juggling several clients.

Can I speak about HR if I am a manager, not an HR professional?

Yes. Many organisers want the manager's view of hiring, onboarding or hybrid work alongside HR's.
