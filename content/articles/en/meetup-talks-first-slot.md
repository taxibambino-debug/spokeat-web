# Meetup talks: how to land your first slot

> **Written by** [Ted Scheiman](/authors/ted-scheiman), banking and fintech executive, guest lecturer. Last reviewed 16 November 2026. [How we research and fact-check](/about/editorial-policy)

If you have never given a talk outside your own company, a conference keynote is the wrong place to start. A meetup is the right one.

Meetups are informal, recurring events for people in one field, often hosted at a local company's office [[3]](#ref-3) [[4]](#ref-4). The audience comes to learn from peers, not to judge a polished performance. Many organisers actively look for new speakers, and some say outright that beginners are welcome.

This guide explains what meetup organisers look for, how to find groups that are still active, and how to pitch your first talk so it gets accepted.

## Key takeaways
- Meetups are the lowest-stakes speaking format: short talks, friendly peer audiences and organisers who need a steady supply of speakers.
- Organisers want practical learnings, real projects and demos. They reject sales pitches.
- Check that a group is active before you pitch: recent and upcoming events, not just a large member count.
- Some groups run open speaker calls; others pick speakers themselves. For the second kind, message the organisers directly.
- A meetup talk gives you a recording, a speaker credit and practice you can build on.

## Why meetups are the right first step

Three things make meetups easier than other formats.

**Organisers need you.** A group that meets every few weeks needs new speakers all the time. AWS User Group Stockholm's call for speakers says "Speakers are always welcome!" and that the organisers are "always looking for new speakers and topics" [[1]](#ref-1).

**Beginners are invited.** The same call says talks are welcome "in any format or at any level of expertise" [[1]](#ref-1). Mind the Product, which runs the ProductTank meetup network, tells first-time speakers to "consider starting at a local ProductTank meetup held in over 200 cities", calling it a way to build experience and join its speaker network [[2]](#ref-2).

**The format is short.** A meetup evening usually has a few talks. ProductTank Stockholm's June 2026 event had three talks and a fireside panel in a 6:00 to 8:30 pm slot [[3]](#ref-3). Stockholm MLOps Community's December 2026 "Winterbash" gives six startups eight minutes each [[4]](#ref-4). You do not need an hour of material.

That makes meetups a good first step on a longer path. Our [12-month plan from first talk to keynote](/en/first-talk-to-keynote-12-month-plan) shows how one talk leads to the next.

## What meetup organisers look for

Organisers that publish speaker guidance agree on a short list.

### Real learnings, not sales
AWS User Group Stockholm wants to hear "your learnings, services you are working with, projects, demos" and states that sales speeches will not be accepted [[1]](#ref-1). If you work for a vendor, talk about a problem you solved, not about your product.

### Something the audience can use
Mind the Product looks for "actionable takeaways": tools and strategies people can use in their own work. It also values fresh ideas that challenge conventional thinking [[2]](#ref-2).

### Depth that fits the room
Technical groups expect technical content. Voxel51, which runs AI and computer vision meetups online and in person, says its most engaging talks "blend theory, mathematics, code examples, data insights, and live demonstrations" [[5]](#ref-5). A marketing or product group will want a different kind of depth. Read past event descriptions to see the level.

### A clear, complete submission
When a group has a form, it tells you exactly what to send. Voxel51 asks for a short title, a 4–5 sentence abstract, a 2–3 sentence bio, a profile photo and your preferred event, for a talk of 30 minutes plus Q&A [[5]](#ref-5). Mind the Product asks for your topic idea and, if you have spoken before, a video of a past talk [[2]](#ref-2). Our guide on [what organisers check in your speaker profile](/en/speaker-profile-what-organisers-check) helps you prepare the bio and photo once and reuse them.

## How to find active groups

A big member count does not mean a group is alive. Before you pitch, check three things.

1. **Recent events.** Look for events in the last two or three months.
2. **Upcoming events.** A group with dates already scheduled is planning ahead and may need speakers.
3. **A speaker route.** A "submit a talk" link, a call-for-speakers page or a line inviting speakers to get in touch.

### Where to look

**Meetup.com.** Search by topic and city. Meetup shows you local groups based on, in order of priority, a location you recently searched for, your device location if you allow it, your profile location, and finally an estimate from your internet connection [[6]](#ref-6). If you see groups from the wrong city, set the location in the search bar or update your profile [[6]](#ref-6). Also note that some results are online events, which can be a good first option [[6]](#ref-6).

**Call-for-speakers platforms.** Some groups use tools such as Sessionize to collect talk proposals. AWS User Group Stockholm's call is open from 5 November 2026 to 3 November 2027, with placeholder dates in February, March and April 2027 [[1]](#ref-1).

**Event platforms and company pages.** Some communities list events on Luma or on their own websites. Stockholm MLOps Community asks attendees to register through Luma [[4]](#ref-4).

**Your own network.** Ask colleagues which meetups they attend. Go to one or two as a guest first. You will learn the format and meet the organiser in person, which makes the pitch much easier.

### Examples from Stockholm (checked 16 November 2026)

Member counts and dates change, so check each page yourself.

| Group | Format | Speaker route |
|---|---|---|
| AWS User Group Stockholm | In-person meetups, 2027 dates planned | Open call on Sessionize; "Speakers are always welcome!" [[1]](#ref-1) |
| ProductTank Stockholm | Evening talks plus panel, hosted by local companies | Event page invites people interested in speaking at the next event [[3]](#ref-3); general form via Mind the Product [[2]](#ref-2) |
| Stockholm AI, ML & Data Developers Group | Frequent online sessions; about 1,345 members and 188 past events | Message the organisers via the group page [[7]](#ref-7) |
| Stockholm MLOps Community | Evening talks at host companies, doors 17:00, talks from 17:45; 5,868 members | No open call published; organisers select speakers [[4]](#ref-4) |

The last row matters. Not every group runs an open call. When organisers select speakers themselves, a short, specific message to them is the only route in.

## How to pitch your first meetup talk

Keep it short. An organiser should be able to say yes or no after one read.

A good first pitch includes:

- **A working title** that names one problem or lesson.
- **Two or three sentences** on what the audience will learn.
- **Why you**: the project or work experience behind the talk.
- **Length and format**: "a 15-minute talk" or "a 5-minute lightning talk". Offer to fit their format.
- **Availability**: two or three months you could do.
- **A no-sales line** if you work for a vendor.

### A hypothetical example

For example, a data engineer at a bank might write to a data meetup:

> Hi, I'd like to propose a 15-minute talk for one of your spring meetups: "What broke when we moved our fraud models to real-time scoring". I'd cover the three failures we hit, how we fixed them and what I'd do differently. It's based on a project I led this year. No product pitch. I could do February, March or April. Happy to adapt to your format.

That is enough. Our [guest lecture email templates](/en/guest-lecture-email-templates) show more ways to structure a short pitch.

## After the talk

A meetup talk is small, but it gives you assets you can use for bigger slots.

- **Ask about a recording.** Some organisers ask for a video of a past talk when you apply to bigger events [[2]](#ref-2).
- **Get a photo of you speaking.** It is useful for your speaker profile and future submissions [[5]](#ref-5).
- **Write down the questions you were asked.** They show what the audience cares about and often become your next talk.
- **Thank the organiser.** Ask whether they know other groups looking for speakers.

Then use it. See our wider guide on [how to get speaking opportunities](/en/how-to-get-speaking-opportunities) for the next step.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need speaking experience to talk at a meetup?
No. Some organisers invite talks at any level of expertise [[1]](#ref-1), and Mind the Product recommends local meetups specifically for first-time speakers [[2]](#ref-2).

### How long is a typical meetup talk?
It varies by group. One meetup network asks for 30 minutes plus Q&A [[5]](#ref-5), while another event gives presenters eight minutes each [[4]](#ref-4). Ask the organiser and offer to fit their slot.

### Can I talk about my company's product?
The organisers we checked either refuse sales pitches outright [[1]](#ref-1) or ask for practical, actionable content [[2]](#ref-2). Talk about what you learned from a real project instead.

### How do I know if a meetup group is still active?
Look for events in the past few months and dates already scheduled. A large member count alone does not show activity.

### Are online meetups worth it for a first talk?
Yes, they can be. Several groups run online sessions [[5]](#ref-5) [[7]](#ref-7), which means no travel and a lower barrier to start.
