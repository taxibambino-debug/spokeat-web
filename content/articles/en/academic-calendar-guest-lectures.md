# When to pitch a guest lecture: the academic calendar explained

> **Written by** [Ted Scheiman](/authors/ted-scheiman), banking and fintech executive, guest lecturer. Last reviewed 16 November 2026. [How we research and fact-check](/about/editorial-policy)

You found the right course. You wrote a clear, specific email. And nobody replied.

Often the problem is not the pitch. It is the date. A course teacher who receives your offer during exam marking, or two weeks into a packed course, may like the idea and still have no way to use it.

Universities run on a calendar that is fixed months in advance. Once you understand how it works, you can time your outreach to arrive when teachers are actually planning. This guide explains terms, semesters and study periods, shows real dates from several universities, and gives a simple timeline for when to get in touch. Spoke At shows you which matching courses start soon, but the method below works without it.

## Key takeaways
- Teaching is planned months before it starts. Exeter collected teaching requirements for 2026/27 by 2 April 2026.
- Calendars differ by country and university: terms, semesters, teaching blocks and shorter study periods.
- The best time to pitch is before a course is planned. Our rule of thumb is one term ahead.
- Avoid exam periods, revision weeks, holidays and the first days of term.
- Always check the specific university's published dates. Do not assume one calendar fits all.

## Why timing matters so much

A guest lecture has to fit into a course that already has a structure: sessions, readings, assignments and exams. That structure is set early. Aalto University School of Business notes that only a limited number of courses run at any given time, so not every guest proposal can be taken up [[10]](#ref-10).

The timetable itself is fixed even earlier. At the University of Exeter, departments had to supply their teaching requirements for 2026/27 to central timetabling by 2 April 2026. Draft first-term timetables went to module convenors on 10 July 2026, and students received working timetables in September [[2]](#ref-2). Loughborough University collects programme, module and timetabling information from December to late March, builds the timetable mainly from April to July, and publishes it to students about four weeks before teaching starts [[3]](#ref-3).

A guest slot inside an existing session can often be arranged later than that. But a teacher is far more likely to say yes while they are still shaping the course than after it is running.

## How the academic year is structured

Universities divide the year in different ways. These are the main patterns you will meet.

**Semesters with teaching blocks.** The University of Edinburgh runs two semesters. Each has teaching blocks, followed by revision and exams [[1]](#ref-1).

**Fall and spring terms with half-term courses.** Harvard Kennedy School runs fall and spring terms. Some courses run for the full term, others only for the first or second half (Fall 1 and Fall 2, Spring 1 and Spring 2) [[4]](#ref-4).

**Terms split into study periods.** Swedish universities divide the year into an autumn and a spring term. At Stockholm University, each term is split into two periods and four term parts [[5]](#ref-5). KTH Royal Institute of Technology splits the year into study periods (P1 to P4), each followed by an exam period [[8]](#ref-8).

The practical point: some courses do not run all term. At KTH, for example, study period 1 in autumn 2027 runs from 30 August to 15 October, about seven weeks [[8]](#ref-8). A course that fits inside one period is short. If you miss its start, you may have to wait a full year for the next round.

## Real dates for 2026/27

These examples show how much calendars vary. Always check the university's own page, as dates can change.

| University | Autumn | Spring | Gaps and exam periods |
|---|---|---|---|
| University of Edinburgh (UK) | Semester 1: 21 Sep to 21 Dec 2026 | Semester 2: 11 Jan to 21 May 2027 | Exams 9 to 21 Dec 2026 and 26 Apr to 21 May 2027; spring vacation 5 to 16 Apr 2027 [[1]](#ref-1) |
| Harvard Kennedy School (US) | Classes 2 Sep to 4 Dec 2026 | Classes 25 Jan to 30 Apr 2027 | Finals 9 to 18 Dec 2026 and 5 to 14 May 2027; spring break 15 to 19 Mar 2027 [[4]](#ref-4) |
| Stockholm University (Sweden) | Autumn term: 31 Aug 2026 to 17 Jan 2027 | Spring term: 18 Jan to 6 Jun 2027 | Each term has two periods [[5]](#ref-5) |
| KTH (Sweden) | P1 ended 9 Oct 2026; P2 began 26 Oct 2026 [[7]](#ref-7) [[9]](#ref-9) | Example from 2027/28: P3 18 Jan to 3 Mar 2028, P4 20 Mar to 17 May 2028 [[8]](#ref-8) | An exam period follows each study period, for example 21 to 29 Oct 2027 after P1 [[8]](#ref-8) |

Two details stand out. First, Swedish autumn terms run into January, so a Swedish course "in the autumn term" might still have teaching after New Year. Second, Edinburgh's semester 1 ends with revision and exams in December, so its teaching weeks end well before the semester does [[1]](#ref-1).

In Sweden there is also a long summer break. Uppsala University's summer period in 2026 ran from 8 June to 30 August, outside the ordinary terms [[6]](#ref-6). A pitch sent in July for a course starting in late August may sit unread until the teacher is back.

### Sweden: course syllabi are fixed before applications open
In Sweden, the Swedish Higher Education Authority (UKÄ) takes the view that course syllabi for freestanding courses should be fixed and available no later than the day the course opens for applications [[11]](#ref-11). For spring 2027 courses, applications on the national system opened on 15 September and closed on 15 October 2026, according to Stockholm University [[12]](#ref-12). By then, the course content is set. The session plan may still be open, but the earlier you write, the easier you are to fit in.

## When to get in touch: a simple timeline

This timeline is our own rule of thumb, based on the planning cycles above. It is not an official university rule.

| Course starts in | Best window to get in touch | Still possible, with a flexible offer |
|---|---|---|
| September (autumn term or semester 1) | March to early June | Mid-August to the first week of term |
| January (spring term or semester 2) | September to November | Early December |
| A later study period (for example late October or March) | Two to four months before the period starts | Up to about four weeks before |

**Practical steps:**

1. **Find the course start date.** Check the course page and the university's term dates page.
2. **Count back.** Use the table above to find your window.
3. **Check for clashes.** Make sure your email does not land in an exam period, a holiday or the first week of term.
4. **Offer flexibility.** If you are late, offer a short online slot or a Q&A, which is easier to fit into an existing plan.
5. **Plan for the next round.** If the course has started, ask whether you could contribute next time it runs.

For help finding courses worth timing, read [how to become a guest lecturer at a university](/en/become-a-guest-lecturer-at-a-university). When you know the timing, use our [guest lecture email templates](/en/guest-lecture-email-templates) to write the pitch.

## Periods to avoid

- **Exam and revision weeks.** Teachers are typically busy with exams and marking. Edinburgh, for example, has revision on 7 and 8 December and exams from 9 to 21 December 2026 [[1]](#ref-1).
- **University closures and holidays.** Edinburgh is closed from 23 December 2026 to 4 January 2027 [[1]](#ref-1).
- **The first week of term.** Teachers are busy with new students, registration and practical problems.
- **The summer in Sweden.** At Uppsala, for example, the spring term 2027 ends on 6 June and the autumn term 2027 starts on 30 August [[6]](#ref-6).

## If you still get no reply

Even perfect timing does not guarantee an answer. Teachers have heavy inboxes, and a "no" often means "not this term". Send one polite follow-up after a week or two, then move on. Our guide on [what to do when an organiser does not reply](/en/organiser-does-not-reply) explains how to follow up without being pushy, and when to stop.

## Frequently asked questions

### When do universities plan guest lectures?
Course structures and timetables are set before term starts. Exeter, for example, collected 2026/27 teaching requirements by 2 April 2026 [[2]](#ref-2). Individual guest slots can be added later, but pitching about one term ahead gives you the best chance.

### Is it too late to pitch a course that starts next month?
Not always, but your offer needs to be flexible. A short online session or Q&A is easier to add to an existing plan than a full lecture. If it does not fit, ask about the next time the course runs.

### What is a study period?
A study period is a shorter block within a term. KTH, for example, divides the year into four study periods, each followed by an exam period [[8]](#ref-8). Some courses run for only one period, so check the dates on the course page.

### Do all universities use the same calendar?
No. UK, US and Swedish universities use different structures, and dates differ between universities in the same country. Always check the university's own term dates page.

### What is the worst time to send a guest lecture pitch?
During exam periods, holidays or the first week of term. Teachers are at their busiest then, and your email is likely to be buried.
