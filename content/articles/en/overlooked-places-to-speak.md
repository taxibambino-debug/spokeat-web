# Places to speak that people overlook: Rotary, study associations, lunch seminars and incubators

> **Written by** [Ted Scheiman](/authors/ted-scheiman), banking and fintech executive, guest lecturer. Last reviewed 17 November 2026. [How we research and fact-check](/about/editorial-policy)

When people look for places to speak, they think of conferences and universities. Both are competitive. Meanwhile, hundreds of local groups need a speaker every week and struggle to fill the slot.

This article covers four types of venue that professionals often miss: service clubs such as Rotary, study associations and folk high schools, breakfast and lunch seminars, and incubators. For each, we explain who decides, what the format looks like and how to approach them. The examples are from Sweden, where we checked every source on 17 November 2026, but the same venues exist in most countries.

## Key takeaways

- Rotary clubs meet regularly, and talks are a core part of many meetings. One Swedish district says it welcomes local business owners and public figures as speakers [[1]](#ref-1) [[2]](#ref-2).
- Swedish study associations ran more than 112,000 cultural programmes, which include lectures, with just over 6.5 million visitors in 2025 [[5]](#ref-5).
- Breakfast and lunch seminars run by business associations and universities have short, fixed formats that suit a focused talk [[13]](#ref-13) [[14]](#ref-14).
- Sweden's incubator network is large: SISP lists 29 incubators and 31 science parks, and many run regular talks [[15]](#ref-15).
- In all four, a volunteer or small team decides. A short, specific offer works better than a speaker profile.

## Why these venues are overlooked

They do not publish calls for speakers. Programmes are planned by volunteers or a small staff, often a term at a time, and the request travels through personal contacts. That makes them invisible to anyone searching for "call for speakers", and open to anyone who asks the right person.

They also give you something conferences rarely do: repeat practice in front of a real audience. If you are building towards bigger stages, see our [12-month plan from first talk to keynote](/en/first-talk-to-keynote-12-month-plan).

## Rotary and other service clubs

**Who decides:** Clubs are self-governing. Rotary International's basic facts guide says that because clubs are self-governing, membership varies from club to club. Every club has officers and committees, and meetings can be formal or informal and may include food and drink, speakers or open discussion [[1]](#ref-1). Rotary describes members as people who attend meetings and use their professional skills to make a difference [[3]](#ref-3).

**What the format looks like:** Rotary District 2365, with about 80 clubs and about 3,000 members in Västra Götaland, Jönköping County, Kungsbacka and Varberg, calls weekly meetings the heart of club life. It says talks are an important part of what clubs do, and that clubs welcome local enthusiasts and public figures, including business owners, as speakers. The brochure even asks readers with something to share to get in touch [[2]](#ref-2). Lunch meetings are common. Mariehamn Rotary Club in Åland, for example, meets on Fridays from 11:45 to 13:00, and a weekly meeting in October 2024 featured a guest speaker on an EU programme [[4]](#ref-4).

**How to approach them:** Find the clubs in your area through the district website, then ask who plans the programme. Offer a talk of a set length on a local or practical topic. Do not sell: a club audience is there for the community, not for a pitch.

## Study associations and folk high schools

Sweden has a large adult education sector called folkbildning. Folkbildningsrådet distributes state grants to eight study associations: ABF, Folkuniversitetet, Medborgarskolan, NBV, Sensus, Studiefrämjandet, Bilda and Vuxenskolan. In 2025 they ran more than 112,000 cultural programmes with just over 6.5 million visitors. There are also 152 folk high schools (April 2026) [[5]](#ref-5).

**Who decides:** Local offices and their programme staff, usually together with a member organisation or local association. ABF Stockholm says it arranges hundreds of events a year, including lectures, seminars, book talks and debates, and invites partners to say what they want to do [[8]](#ref-8). ABF says it offers lectures and cultural events in every Swedish municipality and has a route for becoming a cultural programme leader [[7]](#ref-7).

**What the format looks like:** A lecture usually counts as a "cultural programme". Vuxenskolan defines one as someone conveying something to an audience, for at least 30 minutes and at least five people, announced in advance and reported to the local office [[6]](#ref-6). Rules differ between study associations, so ask the local office.

**Where the audiences are:** Study associations often work through member organisations. Vuxenskolan and the pensioners' organisation SPF Seniorerna in Kalmar County publish a joint catalogue of lectures and circles, with topics from AI and digital skills to fraud prevention, planned and documented together under SV's guidelines [[9]](#ref-9). Folk high schools run their own open programmes. Vara folk high school's spring 2026 programme had free open lectures on four dates, two per day, with speakers such as a journalist and a sports club chair [[10]](#ref-10). Some meetups also partner with a study association: a 2019 Uppsala Linux User Group afternoon of talks at the city library was run "in cooperation with Studiefrämjandet" [[12]](#ref-12).

**How to approach them:** Contact the local office of the study association that fits your topic, or a local association that already works with one. Offer a 45 to 60 minute talk with time for questions. Note that the host may cover the practical side: Folkuniversitetet in Umeå's researcher lecture series asks host organisations to provide the venue, staff on site and any travel [[11]](#ref-11).

## Breakfast and lunch seminars

**Who decides:** Local business associations, chambers of commerce and university collaboration offices. These events are planned by small teams who need a speaker for every date.

**What the format looks like:** Short and fixed. Företagarna Lerum's Frukostklubben on 19 November 2026 ran breakfast from 08:00 to 08:30, then a talk and discussion until 09:30. The speaker was the CEO of the West Sweden Chamber of Commerce, and local politicians and municipal heads of department were expected to attend [[13]](#ref-13). Linköping University's LiU Lunchklubben is a monthly lunch forum where university, business and society meet, run with two science parks and the region [[14]](#ref-14).

**How to approach them:** Pitch one clear idea that fits in 20 to 30 minutes. Lead with what a local business owner can use on Monday morning. Our guide to [speaking at industry associations and chambers](/en/speak-at-industry-associations-and-chambers) covers chambers in more detail.

## Incubators and science parks

**Who decides:** The community or events manager. Swedish Incubators & Science Parks (SISP) lists 29 incubators and 31 science parks "throughout Sweden – from north to south" [[15]](#ref-15).

**What the format looks like:** Breakfasts, lunch talks and workshops for founders. LEAD in Linköping and Norrköping lists LEAD Talks, lunch lectures and seminars among past events [[17]](#ref-17). Ideon in Lund had a Tech Hub Breakfast on intellectual property strategy on 19 November 2026 [[18]](#ref-18). Sting in Stockholm runs in-person and online events and brings together "Sweden's most experienced coaches, successful entrepreneurs and dedicated investors" [[16]](#ref-16). Some slots are reserved: UU Innovation's networking breakfast in Uppsala features one of its own alumni each time [[19]](#ref-19).

**How to approach them:** Founders want specific, practical knowledge: funding, IP, hiring, sales, regulation. Offer a talk or workshop on one problem, and accept that a coaching session or office hours may come first.

## A quick comparison

| Venue | Who decides | Typical format | Best first move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rotary and service clubs | Club programme lead or president | Lunch or evening meeting with a talk | Ask a member who plans the programme |
| Study associations | Local office and partner associations | Lecture of 30 minutes or more | Contact the local office with a topic |
| Folk high schools | School staff | Open lectures, often daytime | Look for an open lecture programme |
| Breakfast and lunch seminars | Business association or university team | 20 to 30 minute talk plus discussion | Pitch one practical idea |
| Incubators and science parks | Community or events manager | Breakfast talk, lunch talk, workshop | Offer a session on one founder problem |

## How to pitch any of these

1. **Be local.** Mention your connection to the town or region.
2. **Name the length and the takeaway.** "A 25-minute talk on X, with three things your members can use."
3. **Keep it non-commercial.** Most of these audiences do not want a sales talk, and some organisers say so outright.
4. **Follow up once.** Volunteers are busy. One polite reminder, then move on. Our guide on [what to do when the organiser does not reply](/en/organiser-does-not-reply) has examples.

For the full method, read [how to get speaking opportunities](/en/how-to-get-speaking-opportunities), and if you prefer to work without a bureau, our guide to [finding speaking engagements without an agency](/en/find-speaking-engagements-without-an-agency). Tech meetups are another open door: see [how to get your first meetup slot](/en/meetup-talks-first-slot).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do Rotary clubs pay speakers?
The Rotary pages we checked do not mention speaker fees [[1]](#ref-1) [[2]](#ref-2). Assume the talk is unpaid unless the club says otherwise, and ask directly if it matters to you.

### Can anyone give a lecture through a study association?
Study associations work with many lecturers and partner organisations, and ABF has a route for becoming a cultural programme leader [[7]](#ref-7). Each association has its own rules, so contact the local office first [[6]](#ref-6).

### What is a lunch and learn?
A short talk held over lunch, inside a company, network or university. LiU Lunchklubben, for example, runs lunch, a talk and networking between 11:30 and 13:00 [[14]](#ref-14).

### Are incubator talks a way to find clients?
They can build relationships, but most incubators expect useful content, not a pitch. Treat the talk as a contribution to the community.
