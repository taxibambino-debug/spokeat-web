# How to speak at student societies (finance, tech, entrepreneurship)

> **Written by** [Ted Scheiman](/authors/ted-scheiman), banking and fintech executive, guest lecturer. Last reviewed 16 November 2026. [How we research and fact-check](/about/editorial-policy)

If you want your first talk this term rather than next year, look at student societies. Finance societies, tech clubs and entrepreneurship societies run events all year. They need speakers every few weeks, and they can book you much faster than a university department.

The catch is that societies work differently from the rest of the university. Students run them. Boards change every year. The person who answered you in March may have graduated by September.

This guide explains how student societies are organised, what kinds of events they run, why lead times are short and how to pitch so that a busy student board says yes.

## Key takeaways
- Societies are the easiest way in. Many state openly that they bring in employers and practitioners, and some say they are regularly looking for speakers [[1]](#ref-1).
- Boards usually serve for one academic year [[2]](#ref-2). Contact the current board, and use a shared society inbox where one exists.
- Typical formats are lunch lectures, breakfast talks, panels, workshops and case evenings [[3]](#ref-3).
- Lead times are short. One students' union asks for external speaker requests at least 10 working days before an event [[4]](#ref-4).
- Pitch a topic that helps members with their studies or careers, not a company presentation.

## Why societies are the easiest way in

Societies exist to give members access to the world outside the lecture hall. Many say so in public.

At Stockholm University, the Finance Society of the business student association F.E.St. writes that it brings "employers to campus for interactive sessions, panel discussions, and networking events" [[5]](#ref-5). At Cambridge Judge Business School, the student-run Cambridge Business School Club is described as "regularly seeking corporate speakers and sponsorship for their events" [[1]](#ref-1). KTH AI Society runs educational events, workshops and hackathons for students from all fields who are interested in artificial intelligence [[6]](#ref-6).

Students also value these meetings. In a survey of 114 undergraduates about industry guest speakers in class, students valued speakers who worked in the field, spoke with enthusiasm and candour, answered questions and gave career advice [[7]](#ref-7). The study looked at classroom speakers, but the same qualities make a good society talk.

Compared with a course lecture, a society talk has three advantages for a new speaker:

- **No syllabus.** You do not have to fit a course module.
- **Faster decisions.** A board can agree to a date in one meeting.
- **More formats.** A panel or fireside chat is easier to start with than a solo lecture.

## How society boards work

Most societies are run by an elected committee or board of students. Knowing how it works saves unanswered emails.

**Roles.** Sheffield Hallam Students' Union, for example, requires three core roles in every society committee: president, secretary and treasurer. The secretary books rooms and makes sure union processes are followed when events are planned [[2]](#ref-2). Larger societies often add an events, partnerships or corporate relations role.

**Terms.** At Sheffield Hallam, committee roles last only for the current academic year, and committees cannot make plans beyond it [[2]](#ref-2). Cardiff University Students' Union advises societies to hold annual general meetings towards the end of the academic year, generally between February and April, and the new committee officially takes over on 1 August [[8]](#ref-8). Rules differ between universities and countries, but annual turnover is common.

**Structure.** Large student associations often contain many smaller groups. LundaEkonomerna, the student union at Lund University School of Economics and Management, has around 280 active members in 26 committees and projects [[9]](#ref-9).

What this means for you:

- **Write to the current board.** Check the society website or social media for this year's committee before you send anything.
- **Prefer a shared inbox.** A society address such as F.E.St.'s info@fest.se [[11]](#ref-11) or KTH AI Society's contact@kthais.com [[6]](#ref-6) survives the yearly handover. A student's personal address may not.
- **Time your pitch.** Where the handover happens over the summer, the new board plans its first events early in the autumn term. Late in the spring term, the outgoing board may not commit to dates after the summer.
- **Find the right sub-group.** In a large association, the corporate relations committee or a specific interest group is the right contact, not the overall board.

## What kinds of events societies run

| Event type | Typical length and setting | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Lunch lecture | About an hour at lunchtime, often with food | Career talks, "a day in my job", company insight |
| Breakfast talk or panel | Early morning, small group | Panels with investors and founders, industry trends |
| Panel or fireside chat | Moderated conversation with Q&A | First-time speakers, topical debates |
| Workshop or hackathon | Hands-on, often with a partner company | Technical skills, case work |
| Case evening | Students solve a business case you present | Recruitment-minded employers, consultancies |
| Inspirational lecture series | Solo talk by a founder or leader | Entrepreneurs with a story to tell |

Some concrete examples, checked on 16 November 2026:

- **LundaEkonomerna's Corporate Relations Committee** arranges breakfast presentations, lunch lectures and case evenings with companies. Its lunch lecture lasts an hour, is held at a typical lunchtime and covers how the company operates and what opportunities it offers students [[3]](#ref-3).
- **KTH AI Society** has hosted a breakfast panel on building and investing in AI businesses, with an investment director and a startup CEO, followed by Q&A and networking [[12]](#ref-12).
- **The Entrepreneurship Society at SSE** runs Initiate, a lecture series in which entrepreneurs speak to students to inspire them towards entrepreneurship [[13]](#ref-13).
- **The SASSE Tech Committee** co-hosted lunch sessions with early-stage founders during Tech Days in May 2026, together with the school's Tech Initiative [[10]](#ref-10).

Societies also co-host. The SSE Tech Initiative has held panel events with KTH AI Society [[14]](#ref-14). If you pitch a joint event to two societies, you may reach a larger and more mixed audience.

## Finance, tech and entrepreneurship: what each wants

**Finance societies** focus on careers in finance. F.E.St.'s Finance Society has groups for asset management, equity research, investment banking and a Women's Finance Network [[5]](#ref-5). Topics that fit: how a trading desk or risk team actually works, how payments or fintech careers differ from banking, or what a first-year analyst should learn.

**Tech societies** want practical depth. At the Stockholm School of Economics, the student Tech Committee runs a coding group, a fintech group and an AI Society [[10]](#ref-10). Topics that fit: lessons from shipping an AI product, how a data team is organised, or what changed when your company adopted a new tool.

**Entrepreneurship societies** want honest founder stories. Topics that fit: the first year of a startup, fundraising mistakes, or how you found your first customers. Real numbers and real setbacks beat general advice.

## Why lead times are short, and what that means

Because boards serve for one academic year and cannot commit beyond it [[2]](#ref-2), societies tend to plan term by term and close to the date. That is good news for a speaker who wants to start soon, but it means you need to respond quickly.

The formal minimum can be short. Many students' unions require societies to register external speakers. UCL's Students' Union, for example, asks societies to submit an external speaker request at least 10 working days before the event and to provide full speaker details. Societies should not advertise the event until the request is approved [[4]](#ref-4). At Sheffield Hallam, an external speaker is anyone outside the society who is not a current student or staff member [[2]](#ref-2).

Practical steps:

- Reply within a day or two when a society proposes a date.
- Have a short bio, a photo and a talk title ready to send.
- Confirm the room, time, format and expected audience in writing.
- Expect the date to move. Exams and union approvals can shift plans.

## How to pitch a student society

A good first message is short and includes:

1. **Who you are.** Role, company and one line of credibility.
2. **The topic.** One title, linked to members' interests or careers.
3. **The format.** Offer two options, for example a 45-minute talk or a panel seat.
4. **Timing.** A few possible weeks this term.
5. **Logistics.** Say whether you can come in person, and whether your company might provide food. Do not promise sponsorship you cannot deliver.

For example, a hypothetical product manager at a fintech company might write to a university's fintech group. He would offer a lunch talk titled "What I wish I knew before my first product role in payments" or a seat on a panel about fintech careers, and suggest three weeks in October.

Avoid two common mistakes. Do not send a sales pitch: students quickly see through an advertisement. And do not write to five board members separately. Use the shared inbox or the role responsible for events.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do student societies pay guest speakers?
None of the society pages we checked mention speaker fees. Assume the talk is unpaid unless the society says otherwise. Many societies have corporate partners, F.E.St. among them [[11]](#ref-11), so ask how a society works with companies before you assume anything.

### How far in advance should I contact a society?
Earlier is better, but societies tend to plan term by term. Some unions only require external speaker requests 10 working days before the event [[4]](#ref-4). Contact a new board early in the autumn term.

### Who should I email at a student society?
Use the society's shared inbox or the role responsible for events or corporate relations. Board members change every year, so personal addresses go out of date quickly [[2]](#ref-2).

### Can I speak at a society at a university I did not attend?
Usually yes. Societies invite employers, founders and practitioners from outside the university. The union may need your details for its external speaker process [[4]](#ref-4).
