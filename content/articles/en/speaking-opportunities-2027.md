# Speaking opportunities in 2027: where professionals find them

> **Written by** [Ted Scheiman](/authors/ted-scheiman), banking and fintech executive, guest lecturer. Last reviewed 16 November 2026. [How we research and fact-check](/about/editorial-policy)

If you want to speak in 2027, some of the most important deadlines are already close. Several conferences for May and June 2027 close their calls for speakers in November and December 2026. University courses for the spring term are being finalised now.

This page is a yearly overview of where speaking opportunities for professionals actually come from, with real examples we checked on 16 November 2026. It covers conferences with open calls, and the less visible routes: university courses, alumni programmes, student societies, associations and meetups. Every listing links to the organiser's own page. Deadlines and terms change, so always confirm on the source before you apply.

We refresh this page each year.

## Key takeaways
- Several 2027 conference calls close in late November and December 2026, the first on 27 November 2026. If you want a spring or early summer slot, apply now, and check the date on the organiser's page.
- Most opportunities are not formal calls. Universities, alumni offices, student societies and meetups publish standing invitations that you can use all year.
- Check the terms. Some conferences cover speakers' travel; at least one major fintech event offers selected speakers a discounted pass rather than free entry [[1]](#ref-1).
- Plan university talks around terms and study periods. Spring term 2027 at Lund University runs from 18 January to 6 June 2027 [[2]](#ref-2).
- Listings below are examples, not rankings. They are grouped by type and ordered alphabetically.

## How to judge an opportunity before you apply

Use four questions.

1. **Who is the audience?** Students, peers, buyers or technical specialists need different talks. Make sure your topic fits.
2. **What does it cost you?** Check whether the organiser covers travel, waives the ticket or expects you to pay. If the source does not say, ask.
3. **What is the route?** Use the form, inbox or contact the organiser publishes. Avoid guessing personal emails.
4. **When is the real deadline?** For conferences it is the call's closing date. For courses it is before the term or study period is planned.

## When to look during 2027

- **November to December:** calls for late spring and early summer conferences close. Spring term courses start in January [[2]](#ref-2).
- **February to May:** a good time to pitch autumn courses and societies' autumn plans, before the summer break.
- **August to November:** autumn term starts. Watch for calls for 2028 events. For comparison, Money20/20 Europe opened the call for its 2027 event on 2 November 2026 [[1]](#ref-1).

These windows are a planning aid based on the dates above, not fixed rules. Every organiser sets its own schedule.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where can I find calls for speakers for 2027?
Start with the conferences in your field and check their "speak" or "call for speakers" pages. Many use platforms such as Sessionize, as DevSum and Nordic Tech Days do [[3]](#ref-3) [[5]](#ref-5). Note the closing dates, which were five to six months before the event in the examples we checked.

### Are 2027 speaking opportunities paid?
Most of the opportunities in this overview do not mention a speaker fee. Some cover travel and accommodation [[3]](#ref-3) [[5]](#ref-5), and some offer speakers a discounted pass instead [[1]](#ref-1). Treat these as visibility and credibility opportunities, and check the terms for each one.

### Is it too late to apply for spring 2027?
Not necessarily. Some calls close in December 2026 [[3]](#ref-3) [[5]](#ref-5), brightonSEO publishes no fixed deadline [[4]](#ref-4), and university and meetup routes are open all year. For spring term courses, contact course coordinators as soon as possible.

### How often is this list updated?
We refresh this overview once a year and note the date we checked each source. Deadlines can change after we check, so always confirm on the organiser's own page.
