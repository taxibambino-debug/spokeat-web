# How to become a TEDx speaker (and whether it is right for you)

> **Written by** [Ted Scheiman](/authors/ted-scheiman), banking and fintech executive, guest lecturer. Last reviewed 17 November 2026. [How we research and fact-check](/about/editorial-policy)

A TEDx talk is on many professionals' wish lists. It is a recognisable name, the talk is filmed, and the format forces you to sharpen one idea. But much of the advice online about "getting booked" on TEDx is wrong, and some of it is sold by people who break TED's own rules.

This guide sticks to what TED and real TEDx teams publish. It explains how speakers are actually chosen, what you are not allowed to do on stage, how to find TEDx events in Sweden and elsewhere, how much work a talk takes, and what to do if TEDx is not the right step yet.

## Key takeaways

- TEDx events are organised locally under a free licence from TED. Local volunteer teams choose speakers, not TED [[1]](#ref-1).
- There is no central application. You apply only where a local team has posted an open call [[1]](#ref-1).
- Selection is based on an idea you are working on, "not their story or credentials" [[1]](#ref-1).
- Speakers never pay and are never paid. You cannot promote your business, products or books [[2]](#ref-2).
- TED's sample timeline for organisers starts six months before the event [[4]](#ref-4).

## What TEDx is

TEDx events are independently organised events that use the TED format under a licence. TED's rules say each licence is non-transferable, valid for one year and tied to a city [[2]](#ref-2). There are several event types. A Standard event serves a city or town. A University event is organised by current students, faculty or staff and "engages the college or university's community first and foremost". There are also Youth, Salon, Library, Business and Internal types, among others [[5]](#ref-5).

The type matters to you. A University event will usually look to its own campus first. A Business or Internal event is a private, staff-only event [[5]](#ref-5), so you can only speak there through the organisation that hosts it.

## How TEDx speakers are actually chosen

TED's page on speaking at TEDx is direct: "Speakers are chosen independently by local volunteer TEDx teams alone, not by TED, event sponsors or any other organizations." There is no central application, and TED does not accept speaker applications for TEDx events. If a local team has posted a public application form, you may use it. If it has not, the team is not accepting unsolicited pitches [[1]](#ref-1).

TED also notes that nominating the same person several times does not increase the chance of selection [[1]](#ref-1).

TED's organiser guide tells curators to go out and look for ideas in their community rather than wait for them, and says: "You're actually looking for a person's idea or innovation, not a person." [[3]](#ref-3) The guide also tells organisers to:

- prefer local speakers, and accept speakers from outside the region only with a strong tie to the community;
- vet every potential speaker to check they "legitimately have an idea worth spreading", including checking credentials and asking outside experts where needed;
- avoid repeat, circuit and commercial speakers, and avoid people who have already given a TEDx talk;
- aim for a line-up that is diverse in background and topic [[3]](#ref-3).

In practice, then, you have two routes: apply through an open call when a local team publishes one, or be found by the team because your work is visible in the community.

## What the rules say about you as a speaker

The TEDx rules and content guidelines are strict, and a good team will apply them [[2]](#ref-2) [[6]](#ref-6):

| Rule | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| "TEDx events may neither pay nor charge speakers." | Anyone asking you for money to speak is breaking the rules. TED asks people to report such offers to tedx@ted.com [[1]](#ref-1) |
| Speakers "cannot promote their own products, books, or businesses, or those of a company which employs them." | The talk must stand on the idea, not on your company |
| Talks are "typically under 18 minutes" and "No panels or audience Q&A are permitted." | You need one idea that holds for a short, solo talk |
| Scientific claims should be "based on data that has survived scrutiny by experts in the field." | Be ready to show your evidence |
| "No talks with an inflammatory political or religious agenda" | Advocacy for parties or campaigns is out |
| Sponsors "cannot be speakers" and organisers cannot speak at events they contribute to | Sponsoring an event does not buy you a slot |

The content guidelines add that speakers may never use the stage "to pitch their products or services, plug their books, or ask for funding" [[6]](#ref-6). If your main goal is to generate leads, TEDx is the wrong format. Our article on [paying to speak](/en/paying-to-speak) covers formats where commercial goals are allowed.

## Finding TEDx events in Sweden

TED's advice is to find an event on the TEDx event map, then look up the event's own website or social media for contact details and any open call [[1]](#ref-1). These are examples of Swedish TEDx events we verified on 17 November 2026. They are listed alphabetically, not ranked, and dates can change.

| Event | Type and date | What we found |
|---|---|---|
| TEDxGöteborg | Gothenburg; no upcoming date listed on its page | Has an "apply to speak" route on its get-involved page and states that "Speakers never pay to join a TEDx event" [[7]](#ref-7) |
| TEDxIHGR Youth | Youth, Gothenburg, 27 February 2027 | Held at Hvitfeldtska gymnasiet, open to the public [[8]](#ref-8) |
| TEDxJönköping University | University, Jönköping, 16 March 2027 | Run by students as part of Jönköping Student Union [[9]](#ref-9) [[10]](#ref-10) |
| TEDxKI | University, Stockholm, 18 April 2026 | Six speakers listed, including two Karolinska Institutet PhD candidates [[11]](#ref-11) |
| TEDxSSE | University, Stockholm, 27 April 2026 | Six speakers listed, including SSE students and founders [[12]](#ref-12) |
| TEDxStockholm | Standard, Stockholm, 15 November 2026 | Speaker applications for this event had closed; anyone could apply and no speaking experience was required [[13]](#ref-13) |

Two observations from these pages. First, line-ups are small: the University events above listed six speakers each. Second, University events often feature their own students and researchers, which fits TED's description of the format [[5]](#ref-5). For a professional outside academia, a Standard event with an open call is usually the more realistic target.

## Realistic odds and preparation time

We found no published acceptance rates for Swedish TEDx events, so we cannot give you odds. What the pages show is that slots are few, calls open once a year at most, and teams look for new local voices.

The work is also substantial. TED's organiser guide gives a sample schedule for speakers [[4]](#ref-4):

- 6 months before: thesis and basic outline due
- 5 months: a script or detailed outline
- 4 months: second draft and first rehearsals
- 3 months: final draft and more rehearsals
- 2 months: rehearsals every two weeks
- 1 month: weekly rehearsals
- 1 to 2 days before: dress rehearsal on site

The guide calls rehearsals "non-negotiable" and says speakers must be reachable and take part in check-ins with the team [[4]](#ref-4). Expect to put in many hours over several months, unpaid. Our article on [the science of storytelling in talks](/en/science-of-storytelling-in-talks) covers what research says about structure, if you are working on that part.

## Is TEDx right for you now?

TEDx suits you if you have one idea that is new or argued in a new way, can explain it without your company, and have months to prepare.

It is probably not the right next step if you want to build a pipeline of talks, reach a specific professional audience, or present your product. Other formats build the same skills with more chances:

- **Open calls for speakers** at industry conferences, where expertise and practical experience count. See our [guide to calls for speakers](/en/call-for-speakers-guide).
- **Guest lectures and student societies**, which give you regular practice in front of a live audience.
- **Meetups**, where a first slot is easier to get and feedback is quick.

A TEDx talk can also be one milestone in a longer plan. Our [12-month plan from first talk to keynote](/en/first-talk-to-keynote-12-month-plan) shows how to build towards larger stages step by step.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I apply directly to TED to give a TEDx talk?
No. TED states that there is no central application and that it does not accept speaker applications for TEDx events. Each local team chooses its own speakers, and you can only apply where a team has posted an open call.

### Do TEDx speakers get paid?
No. TEDx rules say events may neither pay nor charge speakers. If someone offers you a TEDx slot for a fee, TED asks you to report it to tedx@ted.com.

### Can I talk about my company in a TEDx talk?
Not as a promotion. Speakers cannot promote their own products, books or businesses, or those of their employer, and may not ask for funding. You can use your experience as evidence for an idea.

### How long is a TEDx talk?
TED's rules say TEDx talks are typically under 18 minutes and most often delivered by one presenter. Panels and audience Q&A are not permitted.

### How far ahead are TEDx speakers chosen?
TED's sample schedule for organisers has a speaker's thesis and outline due six months before the event, so selection usually happens earlier still. Check each event's open call for its own dates.
