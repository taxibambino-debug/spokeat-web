# How to choose a public speaking course: course, club or coach?

> **Written by** [Ted Scheiman](/authors/ted-scheiman), banking and fintech executive, guest lecturer. Last reviewed 16 November 2026. [How we research and fact-check](/about/editorial-policy)

You have decided to get better at speaking. Now you face three very different routes: a structured course, a speaking club, or a private coach. Each one is right for someone. None is right for everyone.

This guide compares the three by what you want to achieve, what you can spend and how much time you have. We use real Swedish examples, checked on 16 November 2026, and we do not rank providers.

## Key takeaways
- A **course** gives you structure and theory in a set time. University courses also give credits and are free for citizens of EU/EEA countries and Switzerland.
- A **club** such as Toastmasters gives you regular practice and peer feedback over months. You can usually visit as a guest before you commit.
- A **coach** gives you one-to-one attention on your own material. It suits a specific high-stakes talk. Ask for a written quote.
- You can combine routes: a course for the basics, then a club or real talks for repetition.
- If fear of speaking feels overwhelming or stops you working, start with your GP or a psychologist, not a course.

## The three options at a glance

| | Course | Club | Coach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What you get** | A planned syllabus, exercises, often an assessed talk | Regular meetings, speaking practice, peer feedback | One-to-one sessions built around your talk, voice or goals |
| **Who gives feedback** | A teacher, sometimes peers | Other members | The coach |
| **Time shape** | Fixed: from two days to a full term | Ongoing: weekly or every other week | Flexible: booked as needed |
| **Proof you did it** | Credits (university) or a course certificate | None formal | None formal |
| **Swedish example** | Lund University's Practical Rhetoric, 15 credits at half pace [[9]](#ref-9) [[10]](#ref-10) | Malmö Toastmasters, every other Monday [[3]](#ref-3) | No example listed: we found no Swedish coach whose own page we could verify |

## Option 1: a course

Courses come in three main forms in Sweden.

**University courses** run over weeks or a term and give higher education credits. The University of Gothenburg's Retorik för yrkesverksamma, for example, is aimed at people whose spoken word is a key work tool. It runs at half pace with afternoon sessions [[5]](#ref-5). The university says its rhetoric teaching combines analysis of speeches with practical speaking training, including managing nerves, voice training and memory techniques [[6]](#ref-6). Linköping University runs its introductory speaking course as a distance course at quarter pace [[7]](#ref-7). A Linköping news article said many participants on its summer speaking course are people in work, not just students [[8]](#ref-8).

**Short courses** from study associations or private trainers last one to a few days, or a few evenings. Folkuniversitetet's Public Speaking in English in Malmö, for example, is four evening sessions [[13]](#ref-13).

**Contract education** is bought by your employer from a university. Lund University says the employer pays and registers you; private individuals cannot buy it themselves [[11]](#ref-11). Linköping University's version alternates lectures with exercises and ends with a presentation and feedback [[12]](#ref-12).

**A course suits you if** you want a solid base, like learning with a plan, or need proof of training.

**It is a weaker fit if** you already know the theory and mainly need more repetitions. A course ends, and skills fade without practice.

## Option 2: a club

Toastmasters International runs a network of speaking clubs, several of them in Sweden. Its club finder lets you search by location, day and time, filter for clubs that accept online attendance, and contact a club or schedule a visit [[1]](#ref-1). Its membership page describes the different roles members take at club meetings [[2]](#ref-2).

Clubs vary. Malmö Toastmasters says it welcomes all experience levels, gives constructive feedback, and that guests can observe without being asked to speak [[3]](#ref-3). Stockholm Speakers Club runs weekly sessions in English and Swedish, offers a mentorship programme and lets you attend as a free guest [[4]](#ref-4).

**A club suits you if** you want steady practice over months, a low-pressure place to try things, and a group that notices your progress.

**It is a weaker fit if** you need expert feedback on a specific talk next week, or want a formal qualification. Feedback comes from peers, whose experience varies.

Ask the club about membership costs before you join. The club pages we checked do not state them.

## Option 3: a coach

A coach works one-to-one on your goals: a keynote, a pitch, your voice, or how you handle questions. Private training companies may also sell coaching. Ask any coach for their background and how they give feedback before you book.

**A coach suits you if** you have a specific, high-stakes talk, want feedback on your own material, or need a schedule that a course cannot offer.

**It is a weaker fit if** your budget is tight or you mainly need more stage time. A coach can prepare you, but cannot give you an audience.

There is no regulated title for "speaking coach" that we could verify. Before you book, ask about the coach's own speaking and teaching experience, how sessions work and the total cost in writing.

## Choose by goal

| Your goal | Start with | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Build a solid base from scratch | Course | Structure and theory in a set time |
| Get comfortable through repetition | Club | Regular practice with low stakes |
| Nail one important talk | Coach, or a short intensive course | Feedback on your actual material |
| Speak with more confidence in English | Course in English or a club meeting in English | Practice in the language you will use |
| Add a credential to your CV | University course | Credits on your transcript |
| Train a whole team | Contract education or an in-company course | Tailored content, paid by the employer |

For example, a hypothetical product manager who must present a strategy to the board in three weeks might book two or three coaching sessions. A hypothetical engineer who wants to speak at meetups next year might take a short course now and join a club for steady practice.

## Choose by budget

- **No tuition:** Swedish university courses are free for citizens of EU/EEA countries and Switzerland. Others pay a fee per course, for example 6,250 SEK for Lund University's 3-credit RETB03 [[15]](#ref-15). Clubs let you visit as a guest first [[4]](#ref-4).
- **Low cost:** short study association courses. Folkuniversitetet's four-session Public Speaking in English in Malmö costs 1,395 SEK as published [[13]](#ref-13).
- **Paid by your employer:** contract education and in-company courses. Ask your manager or HR whether there is a training budget.
- **Higher cost:** private open courses and coaching. Prices vary by provider and are not always published. Ask for a written quote that includes VAT.

## Choose by time

| Time you have | Good fit |
|---|---|
| Two full days | A private intensive course |
| One evening a week for a month | A study association course |
| A few hours a week for a term | A university course at quarter or half pace, on campus or at a distance [[7]](#ref-7) [[9]](#ref-9) |
| Two evenings a month, ongoing | A club that meets every other week |
| A few hours before a big talk | Coaching sessions |

## Questions to ask any provider

1. How much of the time will I spend speaking, not listening?
2. Who gives feedback, and how much of it is individual?
3. How big is the group? Göteborgs Tekniska College, for example, runs its one-day company course in speaking for groups of 8 to 12 [[14]](#ref-14).
4. Will I practise in the language and setting I need?
5. What does it cost in total, including materials and VAT?
6. Can I try a session or visit first?

## Combine the routes

The options are not exclusive. One sensible pattern is course first, then practice. Once you have the basics, the best practice is a real audience: a guest lecture, a student society, or a meetup. See our guides to [public speaking courses in Sweden](/en/public-speaking-courses-sweden), [in Stockholm](/en/public-speaking-courses-stockholm), [in Gothenburg](/en/public-speaking-courses-gothenburg), [in Malmö and Lund](/en/public-speaking-courses-malmo-lund), and [online](/en/online-public-speaking-courses). For credit-bearing options, see [university courses in rhetoric](/en/university-rhetoric-courses).

## When a course is not the first step

Some courses include work on nerves. The University of Gothenburg, for example, lists managing nervousness as part of its rhetoric teaching [[6]](#ref-6). But if your fear of speaking is persistent or severe, or makes you avoid work or daily life, talk to a GP or a psychologist before you sign up for anything. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (112 in Sweden and the EU). Read more in our guide on [when fear of speaking needs help](/en/when-fear-of-speaking-needs-help).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Toastmasters better than a public speaking course?
Neither is better in general. A course gives structure, theory and expert feedback in a set time. Toastmasters gives regular peer practice over months. You can use a course for the basics and a club to keep practising.

### How much does a public speaking course cost in Sweden?
University courses charge no tuition to citizens of EU/EEA countries and Switzerland. Private course prices vary and are not always published. A four-session study association course in Malmö costs 1,395 SEK as published. Check each provider's own page.

### Do I need a coach to give a good talk?
No. A coach helps most when the stakes are high and you want feedback on your own material. For general confidence, a course plus regular practice may be enough.

### Can my employer pay for a public speaking course?
Often, yes. Universities sell contract education that the employer buys and registers you for. Private providers also run in-company courses. Ask your manager or HR.
